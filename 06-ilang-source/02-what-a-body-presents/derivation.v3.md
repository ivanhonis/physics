## Setup and assumptions

**Objects and contract** (question, "Setting"; each an assumption of the package, M8). A Model D body $`b`$ on a chain of $`n`$ position labels (A1), a medium $`c`$, a field object $`f`$ of any finite type ($`d_f=\dim\mathcal H_f`$) with self-adjoint $`C_f`$ and $`\Phi_x`$. Each object is the only instance of its type, so (1.11) imposes nothing and $`\mathcal H_{\rm adm}=\mathcal H`$. The total contract is $`C=C_{\rm D}+C_{bc}+C_f+C_{bf}`$ with $`C_{bc}=\sum_x\lvert x\rangle\langle x\rvert\otimes\kappa xX`$ and $`C_{bf}=C^{(a)}_{bf}`$ or $`C^{(c)}_{bf}`$ as in the question, $`\gamma\in\mathbb R`$.

**Notation.** $`\Pi_x:=\lvert x\rangle\langle x\rvert\otimes\mathbb 1_2\otimes\mathbb 1_N`$; $`O:=\sigma_3\otimes(m\mathbb 1_N+D)`$ on the internal labels; $`A_x:=\frac\tau2\bigl(\lvert x+1\rangle\langle x\rvert\otimes(-i\sigma_1)+\lvert x\rangle\langle x+1\rvert\otimes(i\sigma_1)\bigr)\otimes\mathbb 1_N`$, the bond $`(x,x+1)`$ of the motion term ($`A_x:=0`$ for an absent bond), so $`A=\sum_xA_x`$ and $`B=\sum_x\Pi_xO`$. $`d_k=\frac{2\pi k}{N\lambda_0}`$, $`\mu_k=m+d_k`$. The *rest* $`R`$ is the set of objects other than $`f`$, and $`H_R`$ the sum of the terms not involving $`f`$ (here $`R=\{b,c\}`$, $`H_R=C_{\rm D}+C_{bc}`$). $`\mathring Y:=Y-\frac{\mathrm{Tr}Y}{d}\mathbb 1`$ is the traceless part of an operator on a $`d`$-dimensional space; $`\lVert\cdot\rVert`$ is the operator norm, $`\lVert\cdot\rVert_1`$ the trace norm.

**Inputs** (04-ilang-time/05-moving-clock@v3, quoted in the question), with $`\lvert p\rangle=\sum_xe^{ipx}\lvert x\rangle`$: by (5.5) of 04-ilang-time/05-moving-clock,
```math
C_{\rm D}\bigl(\lvert p\rangle\otimes\beta\otimes\lvert f_k\rangle\bigr)=\lvert p\rangle\otimes C_k(p)\beta\otimes\lvert f_k\rangle,\qquad C_k(p)=-\tau\sin p\,\sigma_1+\mu_k\sigma_3 ;
```
by (5.6) of 04-ilang-time/05-moving-clock, $`C_k(p)`$ has the eigenvalues $`\pm\omega_k(p)`$, $`\omega_k=\sqrt{\mu_k^2+\tau^2\sin^2p}`$, with the real eigenvectors $`\beta_{k,+}=(\cos\frac{\theta_k}2,\sin\frac{\theta_k}2)^{T}`$, $`\beta_{k,-}=(-\sin\frac{\theta_k}2,\cos\frac{\theta_k}2)^{T}`$, $`\tan\theta_k(p)=-\tau\sin p/\mu_k`$, $`\lvert\theta_k\rvert<\frac\pi2`$.

**Half-gap.** $`M_k:=\mu_k=m+d_k`$. At $`\sin p=0`$ the two branches of level $`k`$ have the costs $`\pm M_k`$, so $`M_k`$ is the half-gap of the on-site block $`\mu_k\sigma_3`$. (The question names $`M_k`$ without quoting its definition; this is the one used here.)

**Assumptions.**
- S1. Product start $`\Psi(0)=\Psi_b\otimes\chi\otimes\varphi`$ (for the pair, $`\Psi_{12}\otimes\chi\otimes\varphi`$).
- S2. First order in the coupling at fixed $`\lambda`$, valid for $`\lVert C_{bf}\rVert\,\lvert\lambda\rvert\ll1`$ with the remainder (2.4).
- S3. Normalized plane waves: on a ring of $`n`$ sites (the periodic chain, $`p\in\frac{2\pi}{n}\mathbb Z`$, a chosen description, M8), where (5.5)–(5.6) hold verbatim for $`\lvert p\rangle_n:=n^{-1/2}\lvert p\rangle`$. Items 2 and 3 are derived on the ring; open chains are not treated there.
- S4. Items 2 and 3 at zeroth order in the records: $`\kappa=0`$ or $`X\chi=0`$; for $`\kappa>0`$ a bound is given in Step 5.
- S5. Pair: $`b_1,b_2`$ of Model D type with their own parameters $`(\tau_i,m_i,N_i,\lambda_{0,i})`$, $`M^{(i)}_{k}`$, both on the position labels $`x`$ of the $`\Phi_x`$; $`U=\sum_{x_1,x_2}\Pi^{(1)}_{x_1}\Pi^{(2)}_{x_2}\otimes u(x_1,x_2)`$, $`u(x_1,x_2)`$ self-adjoint on the internal labels of both bodies, with $`[U,D_i]=0`$ so that fixed clock levels form invariant subspaces.

## Derivation

### Step 1. The coupling as a sum over positions

Shifting the summation index in $`\sum_xA_x\otimes\frac12\Phi_{x+1}`$ gives $`\sum_xA_x\otimes\frac12(\Phi_x+\Phi_{x+1})=\sum_x\frac12(A_x+A_{x-1})\otimes\Phi_x`$. Both couplings therefore have the form
```math
C_{bf}=\gamma\sum_xQ_x\otimes\Phi_x,\qquad Q^{(a)}_x=\Pi_xO,\qquad Q^{(c)}_x=\Pi_xO+\tfrac12\bigl(A_x+A_{x-1}\bigr). \qquad\text{(2.1)}
```
Every $`A_x`$ enters $`Q_x`$ and $`Q_{x+1}`$ with weight $`\frac12`$; $`\mathrm{Tr}\sigma_3=0`$, and $`A_x`$ is off-diagonal in the position. Hence
```math
\sum_xQ^{(a)}_x=B,\qquad\sum_xQ^{(c)}_x=A+B=C_{\rm D},\qquad\mathrm{Tr}Q_x=0 . \qquad\text{(2.2)}
```
Let $`\phi:=\max_x\lVert\Phi_x\rVert`$. Since the $`\Pi_x`$ are orthogonal and $`\lVert O\rVert=\mu_{N-1}`$, $`\lVert C^{(a)}_{bf}\rVert=\lvert\gamma\rvert\mu_{N-1}\phi`$. The motion part of $`C^{(c)}_{bf}`$ is $`\frac{\gamma\tau}2(T+T^\dagger)`$ with $`T:=\sum_x\lvert x+1\rangle\langle x\rvert\otimes(-i\sigma_1)\otimes\mathbb 1_N\otimes\frac12(\Phi_x+\Phi_{x+1})`$ (identity on $`c`$), summed over the bonds present. Distinct bonds have distinct target sites $`x+1`$, on every ring and on the open chain. So for $`v=\sum_x\lvert x\rangle\otimes v_x`$, $`\lVert Tv\rVert^2=\sum_x\bigl\lVert\bigl((-i\sigma_1)\otimes\mathbb 1_N\otimes\frac12(\Phi_x+\Phi_{x+1})\bigr)v_x\bigr\rVert^2\le\phi^2\lVert v\rVert^2`$, hence $`\lVert T\rVert\le\phi`$ and $`\lVert C^{(c)}_{bf}\rVert\le\lvert\gamma\rvert(\mu_{N-1}+\tau)\phi`$. Both bounds are independent of $`n`$.

### Step 2. Exact equation of the view of $`f`$

Let $`\rho=\lvert\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi\rvert`$ and $`V_f=\mathrm{Tr}_R\rho`$ (1.5). By (1.9), $`i\,\mathrm d\rho/\mathrm d\lambda=[C,\rho]`$. The partial trace over $`R`$ of a commutator with an operator on $`R`$ vanishes, and $`\mathrm{Tr}_R[\mathbb 1\otimes C_f,\rho]=[C_f,V_f]`$, so
```math
i\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm d\lambda}V_f=[C_f,V_f]+\mathrm{Tr}_R[C_{bf},\rho]\qquad\text{(exact)}.
```

### Step 3. First-order generator and remainder

Let $`\xi(\lambda):=e^{-iH_R\lambda}(\Psi_b\otimes\chi)`$ be the state of the rest at $`\gamma=0`$, $`V^0_b(\lambda)`$ its view on $`b`$, and $`\Delta:=\rho-\lvert\xi\rangle\langle\xi\rvert\otimes V_f`$. Since $`\mathrm{Tr}_R\bigl((Q_x\otimes\Phi_x)(\lvert\xi\rangle\langle\xi\rvert\otimes V_f)\bigr)=\langle\xi\vert Q_x\vert\xi\rangle\,\Phi_xV_f`$, Step 2 becomes
```math
i\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm d\lambda}V_f=[G_f(\lambda),V_f]+R_f(\lambda),\qquad G_f(\lambda)=C_f+\sum_xs_x(\lambda)\Phi_x,\qquad s_x(\lambda):=\gamma\langle\xi(\lambda)\vert Q_x\vert\xi(\lambda)\rangle=\gamma\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(Q_xV^0_b(\lambda)\bigr), \qquad\text{(2.3)}
```
with $`R_f=\mathrm{Tr}_R[C_{bf},\Delta]`$. The family $`s_x`$ is the source field of this listing (what of it $`C`$ fixes: Step 8); its total is $`S:=\sum_xs_x`$.

*Remainder.* $`\Psi^0(\lambda)=\xi(\lambda)\otimes e^{-iC_f\lambda}\varphi`$ is the evolution at $`\gamma=0`$. By Duhamel's formula, $`\Psi-\Psi^0=-i\int_0^\lambda e^{-iC(\lambda-\lambda')}C_{bf}\Psi^0(\lambda')\,\mathrm d\lambda'`$, so $`\lVert\Psi-\Psi^0\rVert\le\lVert C_{bf}\rVert\lvert\lambda\rvert`$. For unit vectors $`\lVert\psi\psi^\dagger-\phi\phi^\dagger\rVert_1\le2\lVert\psi-\phi\rVert`$, partial traces do not increase the trace norm, and $`\Delta=(\rho-\rho^0)-\lvert\xi\rangle\langle\xi\rvert\otimes(V_f-V^0_f)`$ with $`\rho^0=\lvert\xi\rangle\langle\xi\rvert\otimes V_f^0`$. Hence $`\lVert\Delta\rVert_1\le4\lVert C_{bf}\rVert\lvert\lambda\rvert`$ and
```math
\lVert R_f(\lambda)\rVert_1\le2\lVert C_{bf}\rVert\,\lVert\Delta\rVert_1\le8\lVert C_{bf}\rVert^2\lvert\lambda\rvert . \qquad\text{(2.4)}
```
The difference between $`V_f`$ and the evolution of $`V_f(0)`$ by the $`\lambda`$-dependent contract $`G_f`$ obeys the same equation with source $`R_f`$ and starts at zero; unitary conjugation preserves $`\lVert\cdot\rVert_1`$, so the difference is at most $`4\lVert C_{bf}\rVert^2\lambda^2`$. For the pair, $`C_{bf}\to C_{b_1f}+C_{b_2f}`$, $`s_x=\gamma_1\langle Q^{(1)}_x\rangle+\gamma_2\langle Q^{(2)}_x\rangle`$, with $`\xi`$ the state of $`R=\{b_1,b_2,c\}`$ at zero coupling; nothing else changes.

### Step 4. When the first-order generator is a contract of $`f`$ alone

With the spectral decomposition $`H_R=\sum_EE\,P_E`$ (finite, A1), $`\xi(\lambda)=\sum_Ee^{-iE\lambda}P_E\xi_0`$, $`\xi_0=\Psi_b\otimes\chi`$, and (2.3) becomes
```math
G_f(\lambda)=C_f+\sum_\nu e^{i\nu\lambda}\,\Gamma_\nu,\qquad\Gamma_\nu:=\gamma\sum_{E-E'=\nu}\ \sum_x\langle\xi_0\vert P_EQ_xP_{E'}\vert\xi_0\rangle\,\Phi_x . \qquad\text{(2.5)}
```
A multiple of $`\mathbb 1_f`$, $`\lambda`$-dependent or not, drops out of $`[G_f,V_f]`$, so what acts on the view is $`\mathring G_f=\mathring C_f+\sum_\nu e^{i\nu\lambda}\mathring\Gamma_\nu`$, with $`\mathring\Gamma_\nu`$ given by (2.5) with $`\mathring\Phi_x`$ in place of $`\Phi_x`$. Exponentials with distinct real frequencies are linearly independent functions of $`\lambda\in\mathbb R`$ (A9; apply it to each matrix element). Hence $`G_f`$ is, modulo $`\mathbb 1_f`$, a $`\lambda`$-independent contract of $`f`$ alone iff $`\mathring\Gamma_\nu=0`$ for every $`\nu\neq0`$, and then $`G_f\equiv C_f+\Gamma_0`$. Literal $`\lambda`$-independence of $`G_f`$ requires $`\Gamma_\nu=0`$ for every $`\nu\neq0`$, which is stronger (example below). Two sufficient cases for the criterion:
- a stationary start, $`\xi_0`$ in one eigenspace of $`H_R`$; for $`\kappa=0`$ or $`X\chi=0`$ this is $`\Psi_b`$ an eigenvector of $`C_{\rm D}`$, since then $`H_R(\psi\otimes\chi)=(C_{\rm D}\psi)\otimes\chi`$ for every $`\psi`$;
- $`\mathring\Phi_x=\mathring\Phi`$ for all $`x`$ in coupling (c) with $`\kappa=0`$ or $`X\chi=0`$, and any $`\Psi_b`$: then $`G_f\equiv C_f+\gamma\langle C_{\rm D}\rangle\mathring\Phi`$ by (2.2), and $`\langle C_{\rm D}\rangle`$ is conserved. In (a), $`\langle B\rangle`$ is not conserved in general: for a superposition of the two branches at one $`p`$ with $`\sin p\ne0`$ it oscillates with frequency $`2\omega_k`$, because $`\langle\beta_{k,+}\vert\sigma_3\vert\beta_{k,-}\rangle=-\sin\theta_k\neq0`$. With all $`\mathring\Phi_x=\mathring\Phi`$, $`\mathring G_f=\mathring C_f+\gamma\langle B\rangle\mathring\Phi`$ then depends on $`\lambda`$ if $`\gamma\mathring\Phi\neq0`$; for $`\Phi_x=\mathbb 1_f`$, $`G_f=C_f+\gamma\langle B\rangle\mathbb 1_f`$ depends on $`\lambda`$ while $`\mathring G_f=\mathring C_f`$ does not.

*Control.* Take the ring (S3), $`\kappa=0`$, and the start $`\Psi_b=\frac1{\sqrt2}\bigl(\lvert p\rangle_n\otimes\beta_{k,+}(p)+\lvert0\rangle_n\otimes\beta_{k,+}(0)\bigr)\otimes\lvert f_k\rangle`$ with $`p\in\frac{2\pi}n\mathbb Z`$ and $`\sin p\neq0`$. The two components have the costs $`\omega_k(p)`$ and $`M_k`$. The required matrix elements are $`\langle0\vert_n\Pi_x\lvert p\rangle_n=e^{ipx}/n`$, $`\langle0\vert_n\frac12(A_x+A_{x-1})\lvert p\rangle_n=-\frac{\tau}{2n}\sin p\,e^{ipx}\sigma_1`$ (direct computation) and $`\beta_{k,+}(0)=(1,0)^{T}`$. They give
```math
s_x(\lambda)=\frac{\gamma}{2n}\Bigl(\langle\beta_{k,+}(p)\vert\hat Q\vert\beta_{k,+}(p)\rangle+M_k\Bigr)+\frac{\gamma g}{n}\cos\bigl(px-(\omega_k(p)-M_k)\lambda\bigr), \qquad\text{(2.6)}
```
with $`\hat Q=\mu_k\sigma_3`$, $`g^{(a)}=M_k\cos\frac{\theta_k}2`$ for (a) and $`\hat Q=C_k(p)`$, $`g^{(c)}=M_k\cos\frac{\theta_k}2-\frac\tau2\sin p\,\sin\frac{\theta_k}2`$ for (c). Both $`g`$ are positive, since $`\cos\frac{\theta_k}2>0`$ and $`\sin\frac{\theta_k}2`$ has the sign of $`-\sin p`$. Since $`\omega_k(p)>M_k`$, $`s_x`$ depends on $`\lambda`$ for $`\gamma\neq0`$. With $`\nu:=\omega_k(p)-M_k`$ and $`Y_p:=\sum_xe^{ipx}\mathring\Phi_x`$, the $`\lambda`$-dependent part of $`\sum_xs_x\mathring\Phi_x`$ is $`\frac{\gamma g}{2n}\bigl(e^{-i\nu\lambda}Y_p+e^{i\nu\lambda}Y_p^\dagger\bigr)`$. So $`G_f`$ depends on $`\lambda`$ modulo $`\mathbb 1_f`$ whenever $`Y_p\neq0`$ (for example, when the $`\mathring\Phi_x`$ are linearly independent).

### Step 5. Total source of a branch start (item 2)

By (2.2), $`S=\gamma\langle B\rangle`$ for (a) and $`S=\gamma\langle C_{\rm D}\rangle`$ for (c). Both operators commute with the translations of the position label. By (5.5) they act on $`\lvert p\rangle\otimes\beta\otimes\lvert f_k\rangle`$ as $`\mu_k\sigma_3`$ and $`C_k(p)`$ on $`\beta`$ ($`B`$ is diagonal in $`x`$, and $`O\lvert f_k\rangle=\mu_k\sigma_3\lvert f_k\rangle`$). For the branch $`\sigma\in\{+1,-1\}`$, (5.6) gives $`\langle\beta_{k,\sigma}\vert\sigma_3\vert\beta_{k,\sigma}\rangle=\sigma(\cos^2\frac{\theta_k}2-\sin^2\frac{\theta_k}2)=\sigma\cos\theta_k=\sigma M_k/\omega_k`$ (because $`\lvert\theta_k\rvert<\frac\pi2`$) and $`\langle\beta_{k,\sigma}\vert C_k\vert\beta_{k,\sigma}\rangle=\sigma\omega_k`$. For the branch start $`\lvert p\rangle_n\otimes\beta_{k,\sigma}(p)\otimes\lvert f_k\rangle`$ (S3) this gives
```math
S^{(a)}_{k,\sigma}(p)=\gamma\sigma\frac{M_k^2}{\omega_k(p)}=\frac{\gamma\sigma M_k^2}{\sqrt{M_k^2+\tau^2\sin^2p}},\qquad S^{(c)}_{k,\sigma}(p)=\gamma\sigma\,\omega_k(p)=\gamma\sigma\sqrt{M_k^2+\tau^2\sin^2p}, \qquad\text{(2.7)}
```
uniformly distributed, $`s_x=S/n`$. With $`L_b:=\langle C_{\rm D}\rangle=\sigma\omega_k(p)`$, the cost of the body's own term in the start,
```math
S^{(c)}_{k,\sigma}=\gamma L_b,\qquad S^{(a)}_{k,\sigma}=\gamma\frac{M_k^2}{L_b}=\gamma L_b\Bigl(\frac{M_k}{\omega_k(p)}\Bigr)^2. \qquad\text{(2.8)}
```
With $`\kappa=0`$ or $`X\chi=0`$, $`\xi(\lambda)=e^{-i\sigma\omega_k\lambda}\xi_0`$, so (2.7) holds at every $`\lambda`$, and $`G_f`$ is $`\lambda`$-independent (Step 4). On the ring, a normalized packet $`\sum_pg(p)\lvert p\rangle_n\otimes\beta_{k,\sigma}(p)\otimes\lvert f_k\rangle`$ ($`p\in\frac{2\pi}n\mathbb Z`$, $`\sum_p\lvert g(p)\rvert^2=1`$) on one branch and level stays on that branch, and both operators are block-diagonal in $`p`$, since they commute with the translations of the ring. Its total source is therefore $`\sum_p\lvert g(p)\rvert^2S_{k,\sigma}(p)`$, constant in $`\lambda`$. For $`\kappa>0`$ and $`X\chi\neq0`$, Duhamel's formula between $`H_R`$ and $`C_{\rm D}`$ gives $`\lvert S(\lambda)-S(0)\rvert\le2\lvert\gamma\rvert\,\lVert\sum_xQ_x\rVert\int_0^{\lvert\lambda\rvert}\lVert C_{bc}\,\xi^0(\lambda')\rVert\,\mathrm d\lambda'`$, where $`\xi^0`$ is the evolution at $`\kappa=0`$. This correction is of first order in $`\kappa`$ at fixed $`\lambda`$.

**Dependence.** $`S_{k,\sigma}(p)`$ is odd in $`\sigma`$. It depends on $`p`$ only through $`\sin^2p`$, and on $`k`$ only through $`M_k=m+\frac{2\pi k}{N\lambda_0}`$. As $`\sin^2p`$ goes from $`0`$ to $`1`$, $`\lvert S^{(a)}\rvert`$ decreases from $`\lvert\gamma\rvert M_k`$ to $`\lvert\gamma\rvert M_k^2/\sqrt{M_k^2+\tau^2}`$, while $`\lvert S^{(c)}\rvert`$ increases from $`\lvert\gamma\rvert M_k`$ to $`\lvert\gamma\rvert\sqrt{M_k^2+\tau^2}`$. Both moduli increase with $`M_k`$.

### Step 6. Bound pair (item 3)

Take $`\Psi_{12}`$ an eigenvector of the pair contract $`C_{\rm pair}:=C_{{\rm D},1}+C_{{\rm D},2}+U`$ with eigenvalue $`E_{12}`$ (the cost of the pair), on the clock levels $`(k_1,k_2)`$ (S5), with $`\kappa=0`$. The start is stationary, so $`G_f`$ is $`\lambda`$-independent (Step 4). By Step 3 and (2.2), $`S=\gamma_1\langle\sum_xQ^{(1)}_x\rangle+\gamma_2\langle\sum_xQ^{(2)}_x\rangle`$. On level $`k_i`$, $`B_i`$ acts as $`M^{(i)}_{k_i}\sigma^{(i)}_3`$, and $`\langle C_{{\rm D},1}\rangle+\langle C_{{\rm D},2}\rangle=E_{12}-\langle U\rangle`$. Therefore
```math
S^{(a)}_{12}=\gamma_1M^{(1)}_{k_1}\langle\sigma^{(1)}_3\rangle+\gamma_2M^{(2)}_{k_2}\langle\sigma^{(2)}_3\rangle,\qquad S^{(c)}_{12}=\gamma_1\langle C_{{\rm D},1}\rangle+\gamma_2\langle C_{{\rm D},2}\rangle\ \overset{\gamma_1=\gamma_2=\gamma}{=}\ \gamma\bigl(E_{12}-\langle U\rangle\bigr), \qquad\text{(2.9)}
```
where the expectation values are taken in $`\Psi_{12}`$. $`U`$ contributes no term of its own: the binding enters only through the state. The total is exactly the sum of what the two parts present in the joint state, because $`\sum_xQ_x`$ is a sum of one-body operators.

*Control.* Take $`U=u\,\sigma^{(1)}_1\sigma^{(2)}_1`$ ($`u\in\mathbb R`$, $`u\neq0`$, identity in positions and clocks), the ring with $`p_1=p_2=0`$, and levels $`(k_1,k_2)`$. By (5.5), $`C_{k}(0)=\mu_k\sigma_3`$ and $`\langle A_i\rangle=0`$, so (a) and (c) coincide. Put $`M:=M^{(1)}_{k_1}+M^{(2)}_{k_2}`$. On $`{\rm span}\{\lvert{\uparrow\uparrow}\rangle,\lvert{\downarrow\downarrow}\rangle\}`$, $`C_{\rm pair}`$ is $`\begin{pmatrix}M&u\\u&-M\end{pmatrix}`$. Its upper eigenvector $`(\cos\frac\phi2,\sin\frac\phi2)`$, $`\tan\phi=u/M`$, $`\lvert\phi\rvert<\frac\pi2`$, has $`\langle\sigma^{(i)}_3\rangle=\cos\phi`$ and $`\langle\sigma^{(1)}_1\sigma^{(2)}_1\rangle=\sin\phi`$, hence
```math
E_{12}=\sqrt{M^2+u^2},\qquad\langle U\rangle=\frac{u^2}{E_{12}},\qquad S_{12}=\bigl(\gamma_1M^{(1)}_{k_1}+\gamma_2M^{(2)}_{k_2}\bigr)\frac{M}{\sqrt{M^2+u^2}} . \qquad\text{(2.10)}
```
The free parts in the same branch, level and $`p=0`$ present $`\gamma_iM^{(i)}_{k_i}`$ (2.7). So $`S_{12}`$ differs from the sum of the free sources whenever $`u\neq0`$ and $`\gamma_1M^{(1)}_{k_1}+\gamma_2M^{(2)}_{k_2}\neq0`$, while $`E_{12}`$ exceeds the sum $`M`$ of the free costs. The product of the free branch states is a start of the same description, with $`s_x(0)=\frac1n\sum_i\gamma_iM^{(i)}_{k_i}`$; the eigenvector has $`s_x=S_{12}/n`$. So on the ring the difference is a change between two starts at $`\lambda=0`$, $`\Delta G_f=\frac1n\bigl(S_{12}-\sum_i\gamma_iM^{(i)}_{k_i}\bigr)\sum_x\mathring\Phi_x`$, which by (2.12) is fixed by $`C`$ and the two starts. The same block, with $`u=u(x_1,x_2)`$, arises for held bodies at fixed positions (A15).

### Step 7. Replacements that leave $`C`$ unchanged (item 4)

Every operator $`Y`$ on $`\mathcal H_b\otimes\mathcal H_c\otimes\mathcal H_f`$ has a unique decomposition $`Y=\sum_{S\subseteq\{b,c,f\}}Y_S`$, in which $`Y_S`$ acts as the identity outside $`S`$ and is traceless in every object of $`S`$. (Expand in product bases of $`\{\mathbb 1\}\cup\{\text{traceless}\}`$; this is linear algebra, M5.) A term on the pair $`\{o,o'\}`$ has components only for $`S\subseteq\{o,o'\}`$. Let two listings with one term per pair give the same $`C`$. The component on a two-object set $`\{o,o'\}`$ comes only from the difference of the two $`\{o,o'\}`$ terms, so that difference has no two-object component. The single-object components of the differences add up to zero for each object, up to constants whose total is zero. Hence the replacements that leave $`C`$ unchanged are exactly
```math
\begin{aligned}&C_{\rm D}\to C_{\rm D}+Z^{\rm D}_b,\quad C_{bc}\to C_{bc}+Z^{bc}_b+Z^{bc}_c,\quad C_{bf}\to C_{bf}+Z^{bf}_b+Z^{bf}_f,\quad C_f\to C_f+Z^{f}_f,\quad C_{cf}:=Z^{cf}_c+Z^{cf}_f,\\&Z^{\rm D}_b+Z^{bc}_b+Z^{bf}_b=0,\qquad Z^{bc}_c+Z^{cf}_c=0,\qquad Z^{f}_f+Z^{bf}_f+Z^{cf}_f=0\quad(\text{up to constants of total zero}),\end{aligned} \qquad\text{(2.11)}
```
where $`Z^{\cdot}_o`$ is any self-adjoint operator on $`\mathcal H_o`$: a constant, a place-dependent operator such as $`\sum_xz_x\Pi_x`$, or an internal operator. $`C_{cf}`$ is a new $`c`$–$`f`$ term, absent from the listing. In words, the only freedom is to move single-object operators between the terms that contain the object. The two-object components $`C_{\{b,c\}}`$ and $`C_{\{b,f\}}`$ are fixed by $`C`$. By (2.2), $`\mathrm{Tr}Q_x=0`$, so $`C_{\{b,f\}}=\gamma\sum_xQ_x\otimes\mathring\Phi_x`$, and the trace parts of the $`\Phi_x`$ give the single-$`b`$ operator $`\gamma\sum_x\frac{\mathrm{Tr}\Phi_x}{d_f}Q_x`$.

### Step 8. What the total contract and the start fix

*Component listing.* By Step 7, $`C`$ is the sum of its components $`C_S`$, each fixed by $`C`$. Collect them as $`C=c_\varnothing\mathbb 1+H^\circ_R+C_{\{f\}}+K`$, where $`H^\circ_R`$ is the sum of the components on nonempty subsets of $`R`$ and $`K`$ the sum of those on sets that contain $`f`$ and meet $`R`$. In the listing of the question $`C_{\rm D}`$, $`C_{bc}`$ and $`C_f`$ contribute nothing to $`K`$, and $`\mathrm{Tr}Q_x=0`$ (2.2). Hence $`K=C_{\{b,f\}}=\gamma\sum_xQ_x\otimes\mathring\Phi_x`$, $`C_{\{f\}}=\mathring C_f`$, $`C_{bf}=K+Z_b\otimes\mathbb 1_f`$ with $`Z_b:=\gamma\sum_x\frac{\mathrm{Tr}\Phi_x}{d_f}Q_x`$, and $`H^\circ_R=H_R+Z_b`$ up to a constant. The map $`Y\mapsto d_f^{-1}\mathrm{Tr}_fY\otimes\mathbb 1_f`$ does not increase the operator norm, so $`\lVert Z_b\rVert\le\lVert C_{bf}\rVert`$ and $`\lVert K\rVert\le2\lVert C_{bf}\rVert`$. For the pair, $`K=\sum_iC_{\{b_i,f\}}`$, one such term per body.

*Generator.* $`(H^\circ_R,C_{\{f\}},K)`$ is a listing of $`C`$ determined by $`C`$ alone. Steps 2–3, applied to it, give the first-order generator
```math
G^\circ_f(\lambda):=C_{\{f\}}+\mathrm{Tr}_R\bigl((\lvert\xi^\circ\rangle\langle\xi^\circ\rvert\otimes\mathbb 1_f)K\bigr)=\mathring C_f+\sum_xs^\circ_x(\lambda)\mathring\Phi_x,\qquad\xi^\circ(\lambda):=e^{-iH^\circ_R\lambda}\xi_0,\quad s^\circ_x:=\gamma\langle\xi^\circ\vert Q_x\vert\xi^\circ\rangle, \qquad\text{(2.12)}
```
and the remainder (2.4) with $`\lVert C_{bf}\rVert`$ replaced by $`\lVert K\rVert`$ (for the pair, $`s^\circ_x=\sum_i\gamma_i\langle\xi^\circ\vert Q^{(i)}_x\vert\xi^\circ\rangle`$). The right side uses only $`C`$ and $`\xi_0`$ ($`\xi^\circ`$ up to a phase, which drops out). So $`G^\circ_f`$ is invariant under every replacement (2.11), place-dependent and large ones included. In any listing $`L`$ of $`C`$, the terms containing $`f`$ sum to $`C_{\{f\}}+K-Y\otimes\mathbb 1_f`$ up to a constant, where $`Y`$ collects the single-object operators of $`R`$ moved into them, and $`H^L_R=H^\circ_R+Y`$. The generator of $`L`$ (Step 3) is the partial trace of these terms against $`\lvert\xi^L\rangle\langle\xi^L\rvert`$, hence $`C_{\{f\}}+\mathrm{Tr}_R((\lvert\xi^L\rangle\langle\xi^L\rvert\otimes\mathbb 1_f)K)`$ modulo $`\mathbb 1_f`$. Single-$`f`$ operators moved between terms cancel exactly; $`Y`$ changes only $`\xi^L`$, by $`\lVert\xi^L-\xi^\circ\rVert\le\lVert Y\rVert\lvert\lambda\rvert`$ (Duhamel). For the listing of the question $`Y=-Z_b`$, so $`\lvert s_x-s^\circ_x\rvert\le2\lvert\gamma\rvert\lVert Q_x\rVert\lVert C_{bf}\rVert\lvert\lambda\rvert`$, and $`G_f\equiv G^\circ_f`$ modulo $`\mathbb 1_f`$ up to $`2\lVert K\rVert\lVert Z_b\rVert\lvert\lambda\rvert\le4\lVert C_{bf}\rVert^2\lvert\lambda\rvert`$, the order of (2.4). For two starts $`\xi_0,\xi'_0`$ of the rest, $`G^\circ_f-G^{\circ\prime}_f=\sum_x(s^\circ_x-s^{\circ\prime}_x)\mathring\Phi_x`$ is fixed by $`C`$ and the two starts, with no further convention.

*Splits.* A source field is a split of $`G^\circ_f`$ into a start-independent contract of $`f`$ and a start-dependent body part. The *component split* takes $`C_{\{f\}}`$ and $`F^\circ:=\sum_xs^\circ_x\mathring\Phi_x`$, with one part $`F^\circ_i`$ for each body, from its component $`C_{\{b_i,f\}}`$. It is a function of $`C`$ and the start. Its zero is a convention: $`\mathrm{Tr}_RK=0`$, so $`F^\circ`$ would vanish for a view of $`R`$ proportional to $`\mathbb 1`$. Any other split with a start-independent field part differs from it by a start-independent operator $`W`$ on $`\mathcal H_f`$. Every $`W`$ is realized by a listing: the move (2.11) $`C_{bf}\to C_{bf}+\mathbb 1_b\otimes W`$, $`C_f\to C_f-W`$ leaves $`C`$ and $`G^\circ_f`$ unchanged and adds $`W`$ to the body part $`\mathrm{Tr}_R((\lvert\xi\rangle\langle\xi\rvert\otimes\mathbb 1_f)C_{bf})`$ of the listing. For $`W=\sum_x\zeta_x\mathring\Phi_x`$ this is $`s_x\to s_x+\zeta_x`$ and $`S\to S+\sum_x\zeta_x`$. With several bodies coupled to $`f`$, each has its own $`W_i`$, and $`C_f\to C_f-\sum_iW_i`$. Since $`\mathrm{Tr}Q_x=0`$, the listing of the question realizes the component split at first order: its body part is $`\sum_xs_x\mathring\Phi_x`$ modulo $`\mathbb 1_f`$, with $`s_x=s^\circ_x`$ up to the bound above.

*Zero of the body operator.* Consider listings whose $`b`$–$`f`$ term has the form (a), resp. (c), with $`O`$ replaced by $`a'O+c_0`$ and with $`\mathring\Phi_x`$ unchanged up to one common factor. Assume $`\gamma\neq0`$ and $`\mathring\Phi_y\neq0`$ for some $`y`$. Two such listings of the same $`C`$ differ in $`C_{\{b,f\}}`$ by the two-object component of $`\gamma\sum_x\Pi_x\bigl((a'-1)O+c_0\bigr)\otimes\mathring\Phi_x`$, which (2.11) requires to vanish. Taking $`\mathrm{Tr}_f`$ removes the single-$`b`$ part, since $`\mathring\Phi_x`$ is traceless. Multiplying by $`\Pi_y\otimes\mathbb 1`$ then gives $`((a'-1)O+c_0)\otimes\mathring\Phi_y=\mathbb 1\otimes\mathring Z`$ for every $`y`$, for one traceless $`\mathring Z`$ on $`\mathcal H_f`$. Since $`O`$ is not a multiple of $`\mathbb 1`$, $`a'=1`$, and then $`c_0\mathring\Phi_y=\mathring Z`$ for all $`y`$. Conversely, if all $`\mathring\Phi_x`$ are equal, $`c_0\sum_x\Pi_x\otimes\mathring\Phi=c_0\mathbb 1_b\otimes\mathring\Phi`$ is a single-$`f`$ operator, which can be moved into $`C_f`$. Hence
```math
\text{a constant }c_0\neq0\text{ added to }\sigma_3\otimes(m\mathbb 1_N+D)\text{ leaves }C\text{ unchanged (after moves (2.11))}\iff\mathring\Phi_x=\mathring\Phi_y\ \text{for all }x,y . \qquad\text{(2.13)}
```
(2.13) is a statement about $`C`$; it does not fix a split.

*Coefficients.* The numbers $`s_x`$, $`S`$ are coefficients with respect to the $`\mathring\Phi_x`$ of the stated coupling. The rescaling $`(\gamma_i,\Phi_x)\to(\gamma_i/a,\,a\Phi_x)`$, common to all bodies coupled to the same $`\Phi_x`$, changes no term and multiplies every coefficient by $`1/a`$. The coefficients of an operator in the span of the $`\mathring\Phi_x`$ are unique if the $`\mathring\Phi_x`$ are linearly independent; if all $`\mathring\Phi_x`$ equal one $`\mathring\Phi\neq0`$, only their sum is. A shift $`W_i`$ is the same for every start, so it cancels in the additive change $`\Delta F_i`$ of the part of body $`i`$ between two starts, hence in $`\Delta s^{(i)}_x`$, $`\Delta S_i`$ and $`\Delta S_1/\Delta S_2`$. It does not cancel in the change of a ratio, since $`S_1/S_2\to(S_1+w_1)/(S_2+w_2)`$ with start-independent $`w_i`$. Hence, for ratios wherever the denominator is nonzero,
```math
\begin{aligned}&\text{(i) }s_x,\ S,\ s^{(1)}_x/s^{(2)}_x\text{ and }S_1/S_2\text{ read from a listing are not fixed by }C\text{ and the start};\\&\text{(ii) in the component split they are, up to }1/a\text{ (ratios exactly), if the }\mathring\Phi_x\text{ are linearly independent; }S^\circ\text{ and }S^\circ_1/S^\circ_2\text{ are if all }\mathring\Phi_x=\mathring\Phi\neq0;\\&\text{(iii) the additive changes between two starts are the same in every split: }\Delta s^{(i)}_x\text{ and }\Delta S_i\text{ if the }\mathring\Phi_x\text{ are linearly independent, }\Delta S_i\text{ if all }\mathring\Phi_x=\mathring\Phi\neq0;\\&\qquad\text{so is }\Delta S_1/\Delta S_2\text{ where }\Delta S_2\neq0;\text{ hence they are fixed by }C\text{ and the two starts, the changes up to }1/a,\text{ the ratio exactly};\\&\text{(iv) the change of a ratio between two starts, }\Delta(S_1/S_2),\text{ is in general not the same in every split.}\end{aligned} \qquad\text{(2.14)}
```
*Control of (iv).* Take two bodies coupled to $`f`$ (S5 with $`U=0`$), $`\kappa=0`$, the ring, $`\gamma_1,\gamma_2\neq0`$, all $`\mathring\Phi_x=\mathring\Phi\neq0`$. In both starts the bodies are at $`p_1=p_2=0`$ on levels $`k_1,k_2`$ and body 2 is on branch $`+1`$; body 1 is on branch $`+1`$ in the first start and $`-1`$ in the second. At $`\lambda=0`$, $`\xi^\circ=\xi`$, and (2.7), applied to each body of the product start, gives $`(S^\circ_1,S^\circ_2)=(q_1,q_2)`$ and $`(-q_1,q_2)`$, with $`q_i:=\gamma_iM^{(i)}_{k_i}\neq0`$. So, with $`\Delta`$ the value in the first start minus that in the second, $`\Delta(S_1/S_2)=2q_1/q_2`$. The move (2.11) of $`\mathbb 1_{b_2}\otimes w\mathring\Phi`$ from $`C_f`$ into $`C_{b_2f}`$, with $`w\notin\{0,-q_2\}`$, leaves $`C`$ unchanged. It changes $`\Delta(S_1/S_2)`$ to $`2q_1/(q_2+w)\neq2q_1/q_2`$, while $`\Delta S_1=2q_1`$ and $`\Delta S_2=0`$ stay the same.
The relation (2.8) of $`S`$ to the cost holds in the listing of the question and is not invariant: a move $`Z^{\rm D}_b`$ between $`C_{\rm D}`$ and $`C_{bc}`$, a constant included, changes $`L_b`$ but not $`S`$, and a move $`\mathbb 1_b\otimes W`$ changes $`S`$ but not $`L_b`$. This is consistent with M6: the cost of a single object is not well defined.

## Result

Class throughout: the setting of the question (Model D body, medium with $`K_x=\kappa xX`$, field object of any finite type with any $`C_f`$ and $`\Phi_x`$, coupling (a) or (c)); further conditions are stated per item. All of these are chosen descriptions (M8). The values of the source field, its total and their ratios in items 2 and 5–9 are those of the listing of the question; items 11 and 13 state what of them $`C`$ and the starts fix.

1. **(2.1), (2.2)** (definition, exact). Both couplings are $`C_{bf}=\gamma\sum_xQ_x\otimes\Phi_x`$ with $`Q^{(a)}_x=\Pi_xO`$ and $`Q^{(c)}_x=\Pi_xO+\frac12(A_x+A_{x-1})`$. They satisfy $`\sum_xQ^{(a)}_x=B`$, $`\sum_xQ^{(c)}_x=C_{\rm D}`$ and $`\mathrm{Tr}Q_x=0`$.
2. **(2.3)** (formula; every product start $`\Psi_b\otimes\chi\otimes\varphi`$; first order in $`\gamma`$ at fixed $`\lambda`$). The view of $`f`$ obeys $`i\,\mathrm dV_f/\mathrm d\lambda=[G_f(\lambda),V_f]+R_f`$, with $`G_f=C_f+\sum_xs_x(\lambda)\Phi_x`$. The source field of the listing of the question is $`s_x(\lambda)=\gamma\langle\xi(\lambda)\vert Q_x\vert\xi(\lambda)\rangle`$, where $`\xi(\lambda)=e^{-iH_R\lambda}(\Psi_b\otimes\chi)`$ is the state of the rest at zero coupling. For the pair, $`s_x=\gamma_1\langle Q^{(1)}_x\rangle+\gamma_2\langle Q^{(2)}_x\rangle`$.

The first-order generator is accurate for $`\lVert C_{bf}\rVert\lvert\lambda\rvert\ll1`$.

The criterion holds for every start in one eigenspace of $`H_R`$ (for $`\kappa=0`$ or $`X\chi=0`$: $`\Psi_b`$ an eigenvector of $`C_{\rm D}`$). In coupling (c) with $`\mathring\Phi_x=\mathring\Phi`$ for all $`x`$ and $`\kappa=0`$ or $`X\chi=0`$, it holds for every $`\Psi_b`$.
5. **(2.6)** (example, control of item 1; $`\gamma\neq0`$). On a ring with $`\kappa=0`$, take the start $`\frac1{\sqrt2}(\lvert p\rangle_n\beta_{k,+}(p)+\lvert0\rangle_n\beta_{k,+}(0))\lvert f_k\rangle`$ with $`\sin p\neq0`$. Its source field oscillates with frequency $`\omega_k(p)-M_k`$ in both couplings, so $`G_f`$ depends on $`\lambda`$ modulo $`\mathbb 1_f`$ whenever $`\sum_xe^{ipx}\mathring\Phi_x\neq0`$.

For $`\gamma\neq0`$, as $`\sin^2p`$ goes from $`0`$ to $`1`$, $`\lvert S^{(a)}\rvert`$ decreases from $`\lvert\gamma\rvert M_k`$, while $`\lvert S^{(c)}\rvert`$ increases from $`\lvert\gamma\rvert M_k`$. Both depend on $`k`$ only through $`M_k`$, and their moduli increase with it. For a packet on one branch and level on the ring, $`S`$ is the $`\lvert g(p)\rvert^2`$-weighted sum of (2.7) over $`p\in\frac{2\pi}n\mathbb Z`$. For $`\kappa>0`$, $`X\chi\neq0`$, the correction is of first order in $`\kappa`$ at fixed $`\lambda`$, with the bound of Step 5.
7. **(2.8)** (formula; same conditions as (2.7)). $`S^{(c)}=\gamma L_b`$ and $`S^{(a)}=\gamma M_k^2/L_b`$, with $`L_b=\langle C_{\rm D}\rangle=\sigma\omega_k(p)`$. This relation holds in the listing of the question and is not invariant: moving a single-body operator $`Z^{\rm D}_b`$ between $`C_{\rm D}`$ and $`C_{bc}`$ changes $`L_b`$ by $`\langle Z^{\rm D}_b\rangle`$ but not $`S`$, and moving $`\mathbb 1_b\otimes\sum_x\zeta_x\mathring\Phi_x`$ from $`C_f`$ into $`C_{bf}`$ changes $`S`$ by $`\sum_x\zeta_x`$ but not $`L_b`$.

The binding term contributes no term of its own, and the total is exactly the sum of what the parts present in the joint state.
9. **(2.10)** (example, control of item 3; $`U=u\sigma^{(1)}_1\sigma^{(2)}_1`$, $`p_1=p_2=0`$ on a ring, or held bodies). The pair presents $`(\gamma_1M^{(1)}_{k_1}+\gamma_2M^{(2)}_{k_2})M/\sqrt{M^2+u^2}`$. This differs from the sum of the free sources whenever $`u\neq0`$ and $`\gamma_1M^{(1)}_{k_1}+\gamma_2M^{(2)}_{k_2}\neq0`$, while its cost $`\sqrt{M^2+u^2}`$ exceeds $`M`$. On the ring the difference is a change between two starts of the same description at $`\lambda=0`$; it reaches $`f`$ as $`\frac1n\bigl(S_{12}-\sum_i\gamma_iM^{(i)}_{k_i}\bigr)\sum_x\mathring\Phi_x`$, fixed by $`C`$ and the two starts.
10. **(2.11)** (theorem; whole setting). The replacements that leave $`C`$ unchanged are exactly the moves of single-object operators (constants, place-dependent and internal operators) between the terms that contain the object, including a new $`c`$–$`f`$ term. The two-object components, in particular $`C_{\{b,f\}}=\gamma\sum_xQ_x\otimes\mathring\Phi_x`$, are fixed by $`C`$.
11. **(2.12)** (theorem; whole setting; first order in $`\gamma`$). The component listing $`(H^\circ_R,C_{\{f\}},K)`$ is fixed by $`C`$. Its first-order generator $`G^\circ_f(\lambda)=\mathring C_f+\sum_xs^\circ_x(\lambda)\mathring\Phi_x`$, with $`s^\circ_x=\gamma\langle\xi^\circ\vert Q_x\vert\xi^\circ\rangle`$ and $`\xi^\circ`$ evolved by $`H^\circ_R`$, is a function of $`C`$ and the start, hence invariant under every replacement (2.11). The view of $`f`$ follows it with the remainder (2.4), $`\lVert C_{bf}\rVert`$ replaced by $`\lVert K\rVert\le2\lVert C_{bf}\rVert`$. The generator (2.3) of the listing of the question equals it modulo $`\mathbb 1_f`$ up to $`4\lVert C_{bf}\rVert^2\lvert\lambda\rvert`$ in operator norm. The change between two starts, $`\sum_x(s^\circ_x-s^{\circ\prime}_x)\mathring\Phi_x`$, is fixed by $`C`$ and the two starts.
12. **(2.13)** (criterion; couplings (a), (c) with $`\gamma\neq0`$ and some $`\mathring\Phi_y\neq0`$). A constant added to $`\sigma_3\otimes(m\mathbb 1_N+D)`$ is a convention iff all $`\mathring\Phi_x`$ are equal (the field does not resolve positions). Otherwise the zero of the body operator is fixed by $`C`$.
13. **(2.14)** (criterion; whole setting; first order in $`\gamma`$; ratios where the denominator is nonzero). (i) The source field, its total and its ratio to another body coupled to the same $`\Phi_x`$, read from a listing, are not fixed by $`C`$ and the start: the move (2.11) of $`\mathbb 1_b\otimes W`$ from $`C_f`$ into $`C_{bf}`$, with any self-adjoint $`W`$ on $`\mathcal H_f`$ chosen for each body, adds $`W`$ to the body part, and $`s_x\to s_x+\zeta_x`$ for $`W=\sum_x\zeta_x\mathring\Phi_x`$. In it, the coefficients $`s^\circ_x`$ with respect to the stated $`\mathring\Phi_x`$, their total $`S^\circ`$ and the ratios between bodies are fixed by $`C`$ and the start, up to the common factor $`1/a`$ (ratios exactly), if the $`\mathring\Phi_x`$ are linearly independent; if all $`\mathring\Phi_x`$ equal one $`\mathring\Phi\neq0`$, $`S^\circ`$ and $`S^\circ_1/S^\circ_2`$ are. Hence they are fixed by $`C`$ and the two starts, the changes up to $`1/a`$, the ratio exactly. ## Consistency checks

1. **Dimensions.** $`\tau`$, $`m`$, $`d_k`$, $`\omega_k`$ and $`C`$ have the unit of $`1/\lambda`$. $`s_x\Phi_x`$ has the unit of $`\gamma\Phi_x\cdot O`$, the same as $`C_{bf}`$, so $`G_f`$ has the unit of $`C`$. In (2.4), $`\lVert C_{bf}\rVert^2\lvert\lambda\rvert`$ has the unit of $`C`$, like $`[G_f,V_f]`$. In (2.7)–(2.10), $`S`$ has the unit of $`\gamma M_k`$. Passed.
2. **Body without motion, $`\tau\to0`$.** $`\omega_k\to M_k`$, $`\theta_k\to0`$ and $`Q^{(c)}_x\to Q^{(a)}_x`$. Both lines of (2.7) give $`\gamma\sigma M_k`$, (2.8) gives $`\gamma M_k^2/L_b=\gamma L_b`$, and the two forms of (2.9) coincide. Passed.
3. **Sum over both branches.** $`S_{k,+}(p)+S_{k,-}(p)=0`$ in both couplings. This equals $`\gamma`$ times the trace of the $`2\times2`$ block ($`\mu_k\sigma_3`$ resp. $`C_k(p)`$) and agrees with $`\mathrm{Tr}Q_x=0`$ in (2.2). Passed.

## Open issues

- $`M_k`$ is taken as $`\mu_k=m+d_k`$, the half-gap of the on-site block; the question does not quote its definition.
- Items 2 and 3 are derived on rings of $`n`$ sites; open chains and their boundary corrections are not treated.
- With records ($`\kappa>0`$, $`X\chi\neq0`$) only a bound on the change of the total source is given; the $`O(\kappa)`$ correction itself is not computed.
- The source field, its total and their ratios are values of a split; the listing of the question realizes the component split, a convention computed from $`C`$. Whether a later package singles out a split, and which field descriptions fix the common factor $`1/a`$ (normalization of the $`\Phi_x`$), are left to the packages on the field's response and profile.
- For the pair, only eigenvectors of the pair contract and one control are treated; there is no general relation between the pair's source and those of its free parts.

## Methods used

- von Neumann equation for the view (partial trace)
- first-order (mean-field) perturbation theory at fixed $`\lambda`$; Duhamel formula; trace-norm bounds
- spectral decomposition; linear independence of exponentials
- plane-wave block diagonalization; two-level eigenproblem
- decomposition of operators on tensor products into traceless components; component listing
- weighted-shift operator-norm estimate
