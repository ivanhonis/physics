## Derivation

### Step 1. The field alone (item 1(a), standard)

$`J>0`$: the graph is connected and bipartite with classes of equal size $`N/2`$. By the Marshall–Lieb–Mattis theorem (Perron–Frobenius after the Marshall sign change in each sector of $`\sum_xS^3_x`$, and Lieb–Mattis ordering) the lowest eigenvalue is simple and its eigenvector has total spin $`\lvert SN/2-SN/2\rvert=0`$. A total-spin singlet satisfies $`U\lvert\Psi_0\rangle=\lvert\Psi_0\rangle`$ for every common rotation, and the expectation of the vector operator $`\mathbf S_x`$ vanishes in it:

```math
J>0:\quad \lvert\Psi_0\rangle\ \text{unique},\qquad U(\theta,\mathbf u)\lvert\Psi_0\rangle=\lvert\Psi_0\rangle\ \ \forall\,\theta,\mathbf u,\qquad \langle\mathbf S_x\rangle=0,\quad\text{both graphs, every }S.\qquad\text{(8.1)}
```

$`J<0`$: the eigenvalues of $`\mathbf S_x\cdot\mathbf S_y=\frac12[(\mathbf S_x+\mathbf S_y)^2-2S(S+1)]`$ are $`\le S^2`$, with equality only on the pair spin $`2S`$; so $`C_{\rm ex}\ge JS^2DN`$ with equality exactly on the multiplet of total spin $`NS`$ (connected graph):

```math
J<0:\quad \text{lowest eigenvalue }JS^2DN,\ (2NS+1)\text{-fold};\ U\ \text{acts irreducibly on it, so no lowest eigenvector is unchanged by all }U.\qquad\text{(8.2)}
```

### Step 2. Exact statements with the push

$`\mathbf n\cdot\sum_x\mathbf S_x`$ commutes with $`C=C_{\rm ex}-h_p\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf S_h`$.

(a) $`J<0`$. $`C\ge JS^2DN-h_pS`$, since $`-h_p\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf S_h\ge-h_pS`$. The product state $`\lvert F\rangle`$ with $`\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf S_x=S`$ at every $`x`$ attains the bound; any state attaining it lies in the multiplet of spin $`NS`$ and has $`\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf S_h=S`$, which in that multiplet leaves only $`\lvert F\rangle`$:

```math
J<0,\ h_p>0:\quad \lvert\Psi_0\rangle=\lvert F\rangle\ \text{unique},\qquad \langle\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf S_x\rangle=S\ \text{exactly, every }x,L,S,h_p>0,\ \text{both graphs and both orders of limits}.\qquad\text{(8.3)}
```

(b) $`J>0`$, fixed $`L`$. At $`h_p=0`$ the lowest eigenvalue is simple (8.1) and isolated (finite dimension, A1), so the lowest eigenvector is analytic in $`h_p`$ near $`0`$ (standard perturbation theory) and $`m(x)=h_p\chi_L(x)+O(h_p^2)`$, $`\chi_L(x)=2\sum_{k\ne0}\epsilon_x\langle0\vert\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf S_x\vert k\rangle\langle k\vert\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf S_h\vert0\rangle/(E_k-E_0)`$, finite for each $`L`$:

```math
J>0:\qquad \lim_{L\to\infty}\,\lim_{h_p\to0}m(x)=0\qquad\text{every }S,\ \text{both graphs}.\qquad\text{(8.4)}
```

### Step 3. Expansion in $`1/S`$ about the orientation set by the push

The product state $`\mathbf S_x=\epsilon_xS\mathbf n`$ minimizes every link term classically, and the push selects $`\epsilon_h=+1`$. Holstein–Primakoff with a rotation by $`\pi`$ about $`\mathbf e_1`$ on class $`-`$: $`\epsilon_x\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf S_x=S-n_x`$, $`\mathbf e_1\cdot\mathbf S_x=\sqrt S\,Q_x+O(S^{-1/2})`$, $`\mathbf e_2\cdot\mathbf S_x=\epsilon_x\sqrt S\,P_x+O(S^{-1/2})`$, with $`[Q_x,P_y]=i\delta_{xy}`$, $`n_x=\frac12(Q_x^2+P_x^2-1)`$. Define the staggered transverse components

```math
q^{(1)}_x:=\epsilon_x\frac{\mathbf e_1\cdot\mathbf S_x}{\sqrt S}=\epsilon_xQ_x,\qquad q^{(2)}_x:=\epsilon_x\frac{\mathbf e_2\cdot\mathbf S_x}{\sqrt S}=P_x,\qquad [q^{(1)}_x,q^{(2)}_y]=i\epsilon_x\delta_{xy}.
```

For a link, $`\mathbf S_x\cdot\mathbf S_y=-(S-n_x)(S-n_y)+S(Q_xQ_y-P_xP_y)+O(1)=-S(S+1)+\frac S2\sum_\alpha(q^{(\alpha)}_x-q^{(\alpha)}_y)^2+O(1)`$, using $`\epsilon_y=-\epsilon_x`$; the push gives $`-h_p(S+\frac12)+\frac{h_p}2\sum_\alpha(q^{(\alpha)}_h)^2`$. Hence

```math
C=E_0+\frac12\sum_{\alpha=1,2}q^{(\alpha){\rm T}}V_a\,q^{(\alpha)}+C_4,\qquad V_a:=JS\Delta+h_pP_h,\qquad E_0=-JS(S+1)DN-h_p(S+\tfrac12),\qquad\text{(8.5)}
```

with $`C_4=O(J)`$ (quartic), of relative order $`1/S`$ while $`\langle n_x\rangle\ll S`$. With the canonical pair $`\tilde Q:=q^{(1)}`$, $`\tilde P:=Eq^{(2)}=\mathbf e_2\cdot\mathbf S/\sqrt S`$ the quadratic part reads $`C_2=\frac12\tilde P^{\rm T}T_a\tilde P+\frac12\tilde Q^{\rm T}V_a\tilde Q`$, $`T_a:=EV_aE=JS\Delta^++h_pP_h`$. For $`h_p>0`$ both $`V_a`$ and $`T_a`$ are positive definite, so $`C_2`$ has a unique Gaussian lowest eigenvector; for $`h_p=0`$ each has one zero mode (the constants, resp. $`\epsilon`$), the two common rotations about $`\mathbf e_1,\mathbf e_2`$. The canonical map $`\tilde Q\to E\tilde P`$, $`\tilde P\to-E\tilde Q`$ leaves $`C_2`$ unchanged, so $`\langle(q^{(1)}_x)^2\rangle=\langle(q^{(2)}_x)^2\rangle`$.

### Step 4. Orientation far from the push (item 1(b), orders $`S`$ and $`S^0`$)

From Step 3, at fixed $`L`$ and fixed $`\eta`$ (the push scaled with $`S`$ as $`h_p=\eta JS`$), $`m(x)=S-\Delta S(x)+O(1/S)`$ with $`\Delta S(x)=\langle n_x\rangle=\langle\tilde Q_x^2\rangle-\frac12`$. The Gaussian covariance $`\langle\tilde Q\tilde Q^{\rm T}\rangle=\frac12T_a^{1/2}(T_a^{1/2}V_aT_a^{1/2})^{-1/2}T_a^{1/2}`$ depends only on $`\eta`$ and the graph, so $`\Delta S(x)`$ is of order $`S^0`$. Leading order: $`m(x)=S`$.

*Zero modes.* The soft mode of $`V_a`$ is the collective rotation. Its eigenvalue $`\lambda_0`$ solves $`1=-h_p[(JS\Delta-\lambda_0)^{-1}]_{hh}=h_p/(N\lambda_0)-h_p\hat g_L(0)/(JS)+O\bigl(h_p\lambda_0(JS)^{-2}N^{-1}\sum_{k\ne0}\lambda_k^{-2}\bigr)`$ (rank-one perturbation; $`\hat g_L`$ is the pseudo-inverse of $`\Delta`$ on the torus, $`\lambda_k`$ its eigenvalues). So $`\lambda_0\simeq h_{\rm eff}/N`$ with

```math
h_{\rm eff}:=\Bigl(\frac1{h_p}+\frac{\hat g_L(0)}{JS}\Bigr)^{-1}\ \longrightarrow\ \Bigl(\frac1{h_p}+\frac{\gamma_3}{JS}\Bigr)^{-1}=\frac{h_p}{1+\eta\gamma_3}\qquad(\text{cubic torus},\ L\to\infty):
```

the field relaxes near the push, and this lowers the restoring coefficient of the uniform rotation from $`h_p`$ to $`h_{\rm eff}`$. On the cubic torus the eigenvector is uniform up to a part $`\propto\hat g_L(\cdot-h)`$ of relative norm $`O(\eta_{\rm eff}/L)`$, where $`\eta_{\rm eff}:=h_{\rm eff}/(JS)\le1/\gamma_3`$. Its kinetic weight is therefore $`2zJS[1+O(L^{-2})]`$, and $`\langle\tilde Q_0^2\rangle\simeq\frac12\sqrt{2zJSN/h_{\rm eff}}`$. Its share per place, together with that of the staggered mode of $`\tilde P`$, adds $`\frac12\sqrt{2zJS/(Nh_{\rm eff})}`$ to $`\Delta S(x)`$. Unless $`\eta\gamma_3\ll1`$, this exceeds the unrelaxed value $`\frac12\sqrt{2zJS/(Nh_p)}`$ by the factor $`\sqrt{1+\eta\gamma_3}`$, which does not tend to $`1`$ as $`L\to\infty`$ at fixed $`\eta`$. On both graphs the fluctuation of the soft mode itself stays small only if

```math
Nh_{\rm eff}S\gg zJ,\qquad\text{i.e.}\quad Nh_pS\gg zJ\ \text{ and }\ NS^2\gg z\,\hat g_L(0) .\qquad\text{(8.6)}
```

The second condition is $`NS^2\gg z\gamma_3`$ on the cubic torus. On the ring, where $`\hat g_L(0)=(L^2-1)/(12L)`$, it is $`S^2\gg\frac16`$.

The order $`L\to\infty`$ first satisfies (8.6) for every $`h_p>0`$ (on the ring if $`S^2\gg\frac16`$); the order $`h_p\to0`$ at fixed $`L`$ violates it, and there the exact (8.4) holds instead.

*Scaling of the push at fixed $`L`$.* At fixed $`\eta`$ this share is $`\frac12\sqrt{2z(1+\eta\gamma_3)/(N\eta)}`$, of order $`S^0`$, and part of $`\Delta S`$. At fixed $`h_p`$ instead, $`\eta\to0`$ as $`S\to\infty`$, so $`h_{\rm eff}=h_p[1+O(1/S)]`$, and the share grows as $`S^{1/2}`$. The two zero modes are a collective rotation of the orientation, with mean square angle $`\langle\theta^2\rangle\simeq\frac12\sqrt{2zJ/(NSh_p)}`$ per transverse component, and they have their own power counting. Their quartic terms (from $`-h_pS\cos\theta`$ and from the rotation on the sphere) are estimated to be of relative order $`\langle\theta^2\rangle`$. That is of order $`zJ/(Nh_p)`$ in $`m`$, the order of $`\Delta S`$ itself. At fixed $`L`$ and fixed $`h_p`$ the expansion therefore reads only $`m(x)=S-\frac12\sqrt{2zJS/(Nh_p)}+O(S^0)`$; replacing $`h_p`$ by $`h_{\rm eff}`$ changes this by $`O(S^{-1/2})`$. An $`O(1/S)`$ remainder is not established there. Both collective terms vanish as $`N\to\infty`$ at every fixed $`h_p>0`$ and $`S`$, so the limit below holds in either scaling.

*Cubic torus, $`L\to\infty`$, then $`R\to\infty`$.* Far from $`h`$ the covariance tends to that of the translation-invariant form without the zero modes: $`T(k)=JSz(1+\gamma_k)`$, $`V(k)=JSz(1-\gamma_k)`$, $`\gamma_k=\frac1D\sum_i\cos k_i`$, $`\omega_k=JSz\sqrt{1-\gamma_k^2}`$, and $`\langle\tilde Q_x^2\rangle\to\int\frac{T(k)}{2\omega_k}=\frac12\int(1-\gamma_k^2)^{-1/2}`$ (pair $`k`$ with $`k+(\pi,\pi,\pi)`$). The push enters only through the zero modes and the propagators between $`x`$ and $`h`$, which vanish as $`R\to\infty`$. Hence, for every $`h_p>0`$ (so also after $`h_p\to0`$),

```math
m(x)\to\epsilon_h\bigl[S-\Delta S_3+O(1/S)\bigr],\qquad \Delta S_3=\frac12\Bigl[\int_{\rm BZ}\frac{d^3k}{(2\pi)^3}\frac1{\sqrt{1-\gamma_k^2}}-1\Bigr]=0.0784.\qquad\text{(8.7)}
```

The value ($`0.07836`$) is computed in `code/v5/output/spinwave_tilt.txt`; the same file gives $`\Delta S`$ at the far place of tori $`L=8,12,16`$ with the push, for $`\eta=0.1,1,10`$. It splits $`\Delta S-\Delta S_3`$ into the relaxed collective term $`\frac12\sqrt{2zJS/(Nh_{\rm eff})}`$ and the bulk finite-size term $`B_L`$. $`B_L`$ is the torus sum without the push and without the two zero modes, minus $`\Delta S_3`$: $`B_L=-0.01218,-0.00542,-0.00305`$, independent of $`\eta`$ and $`\simeq-0.78/L^2`$. The rest is between $`+0.00001`$ and $`+0.00011`$ for all nine $`(L,\eta)`$; for example, at $`\eta=10`$ the values $`\Delta S-\Delta S_3=0.03333,0.01934,0.01303`$ face the relaxed term $`0.04546,0.02475,0.01607`$. With the unrelaxed term ($`h_p`$ instead of $`h_{\rm eff}`$) the rest at $`L=8`$ would be $`0.003,0.009,0.021`$ for $`\eta=0.1,1,10`$ (differences of the printed columns), growing with $`\eta`$.

*Ring.* $`(1-\gamma_k^2)^{-1/2}=1/\lvert\sin k\rvert`$ is not integrable; the pinning at $`h`$ cuts it off at the distance $`R`$. With $`T/(2\omega)\simeq1/\lvert k\rvert`$ near $`k=0`$ (and likewise for $`\tilde P`$ near $`\pi`$), $`G(r)=\frac1{2\pi}\int dk\,e^{ikr}/\lvert k\rvert`$; at long wavelengths the pinned place acts as a fixed value, and by the image at $`h`$, $`\langle\tilde Q_R^2\rangle\simeq G(0)-G(2R)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}dk\,\frac{1-\cos 2kR}{\lvert k\rvert}\simeq\frac1\pi\ln R`$:

```math
\text{ring:}\qquad m(R)=S-\frac1\pi\ln R+O(1)+O(1/S),\qquad\text{(8.8)}
```

confirmed in `code/v5/output/spinwave_tilt.txt` (slope $`0.3156`$ of $`\Delta S(L/2)`$ in $`\ln L`$ for $`L=256`$–$`1024`$, $`\eta=1`$, against $`1/\pi=0.3183`$). The first correction is not uniform in $`R`$ and exceeds $`S`$ for $`\ln R\gtrsim\pi S`$: on the ring the expansion in $`1/S`$ does not decide the order $`L\to\infty`$, $`R\to\infty`$ at any $`S`$. The ring is the control of (8.7).

### Step 6. Identification with the oscillator field of 05-anchor (item 2)

*Static identification.* For any positive definite kinetic matrix $`T`$, the shift $`q\to q-A^{-1}b`$ shows that the lowest eigenvalue of $`\frac12p^{\rm T}Tp+\frac12q^{\rm T}Aq+b^{\rm T}q+c_0`$ is $`c_0+\frac12{\rm Tr}(T^{1/2}AT^{1/2})^{1/2}-\frac12b^{\rm T}A^{-1}b`$ and $`\langle q\rangle=-A^{-1}b`$; a lowest eigenvector exists iff $`A>0`$. So every source-dependent static result of 05-anchor depends on $`A`$ and $`b`$ only. A direction source gives, at the order of (8.5), $`g\,\mathbf m\cdot\mathbf S_x=g(\mathbf m\cdot\mathbf n)\epsilon_x(S-n_x)+g\sqrt S\epsilon_x\sum_\alpha(\mathbf m\cdot\mathbf e_\alpha)q^{(\alpha)}_x`$, and a cell $`g\,\mathbf m\cdot(\mathbf S_x-\mathbf S_{x'})=g(\mathbf m\cdot\mathbf n)\epsilon_x(2S-n_x-n_{x'})+g\sqrt S\epsilon_x\sum_\alpha(\mathbf m\cdot\mathbf e_\alpha)(q^{(\alpha)}_x+q^{(\alpha)}_{x'})`$. Comparing with (8.5):

```math
q_x\leftrightarrow q^{(\alpha)}_x=\epsilon_x\frac{\mathbf e_\alpha\cdot\mathbf S_x}{\sqrt S},\qquad \kappa_h=JS,\qquad \kappa_a=h_p,\qquad g_s=\epsilon_{x_s}\sqrt S\,g\,(\mathbf m\cdot\mathbf e_\alpha),\qquad c_0=E_0+\textstyle\sum_s g(\mathbf m\cdot\mathbf n)\epsilon_{x_s}S,\qquad\text{(8.10)}
```

two copies $`\alpha=1,2`$ with the same $`A=V_a`$ (no pinning, the push as the anchor), each with its own sources. The copies are not independent fields: they are conjugate to each other, and as one oscillator field ($`q=q^{(1)}`$, $`p=\tilde P`$) the kinetic matrix is $`T_a=EV_aE`$ instead of $`\mathbb 1`$. *Long-wavelength identification* (needed for dynamics; an approximation, separate from the exact static identification above). $`T_a(k)\to T_0:=2zJS`$ near $`k=0`$ and $`\to0`$ near $`k=(\pi,\dots)`$, where $`q^{(2)}`$ takes the role of coordinate. With the smooth coordinates and momenta $`\phi_\alpha,\pi_\alpha`$ of the two points, $`\tilde Q=\phi_1-\epsilon\pi_2`$, $`\tilde P=\pi_1+\epsilon\phi_2`$ (used again in Step 11), and $`q_\alpha=\phi_\alpha/\sqrt{T_0}`$, $`p_\alpha=\sqrt{T_0}\pi_\alpha`$, the bulk is two copies of the 05-anchor field with $`\frac12p^2`$: $`\kappa_h=2zJ^2S^2`$, $`g_s=\epsilon_{x_s}S\sqrt{2zJ}\,g(\mathbf m\cdot\mathbf e_\alpha)`$, speed $`c=\sqrt{\kappa_h}=JS\sqrt{2z}`$.

*Local terms at $`h`$: matched to the exact static result.* Substituting the decomposition into the push $`\frac{h_p}2(\tilde Q_h^2+\tilde P_h^2)`$ gives the anchor $`\frac{h_pT_0}2(q_1^2+q_2^2)_h`$, a kinetic term $`\frac{h_p}{2T_0}(p_1^2+p_2^2)_h`$ and a mixed term $`-h_p\epsilon_h(q_1p_2-q_2p_1)_h`$. These terms contradict the exact static result. Completing the square in the momenta at $`h`$, together with the bulk $`\frac12(p_1^2+p_2^2)_h`$, gives

```math
\frac{1+h_p/T_0}2(p_1^2+p_2^2)_h-h_p\epsilon_h(q_1p_2-q_2p_1)_h+\frac{h_pT_0}2(q_1^2+q_2^2)_h\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{static anchor}\ \ h_pT_0-\frac{h_p^2}{1+h_p/T_0}=\frac{h_pT_0}{1+h_p/T_0}.
```

In $`\tilde Q,\tilde P`$, however, $`C_2`$ has no coordinate–momentum cross term, so the shift of the static identification is exact. The static matrix of $`q_\alpha=q^{(\alpha)}/\sqrt{T_0}`$ is $`T_0V_a=\kappa_h\Delta+h_pT_0P_h`$, with anchor $`h_pT_0`$. The substituted terms are thus off by the factor $`(1+\eta/(2z))^{-1}`$ at zero frequency, which no small wave number removes. The cause is a double counting: $`q_1=\tilde Q/\sqrt{T_0}`$ on the whole lattice already carries the whole static susceptibility of $`\tilde Q_h`$, its staggered part included, and the substitution adds that staggered part again as the momentum $`\pi_2(h)`$. We therefore match the local coefficients to the exact static result. The *long-wavelength model* is the two copies with $`\frac12p^2`$ and $`\kappa_h\Delta`$ on the whole lattice, with the anchor $`\frac{\kappa_a}2(q_1^2+q_2^2)_h`$, $`\kappa_a=2zJSh_p`$, and without local momentum terms. Its static matrix is $`T_0V_a`$, so it reproduces exactly, for every $`\eta`$ and $`L`$, the static results of (8.5) with linear sources: the source-dependent part of the lowest eigenvalue and $`\langle q\rangle`$.

*Range for dynamics* (cubic torus, $`D=3`$). The static matching does not fix the frequency dependence at $`h`$. We bound it using that the push is exactly a perturbation of one site. Take the order $`L\to\infty`$ first and an imaginary frequency $`\nu\ne0`$, and let $`G^0`$ be the imaginary-frequency propagators without push. ### Step 9. Sources quadratic in the deviations: the second-order formula

Along-$`\mathbf n`$ sources and relation sources add, at the order of (8.5), local quadratic terms $`\delta H_i=\frac12\tilde Q^{\rm T}A_i\tilde Q+\frac12\tilde P^{\rm T}B_i\tilde P`$ and constants linear in the strengths.
```math
\mathcal E_{12}=-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\tau\,\langle T\,\delta H_1(\tau)\,\delta H_2(0)\rangle_c .\qquad\text{(8.15)}
```

At large separation only the low modes matter: per copy, the coordinate ($`q^{(1)}`$ near $`k=0`$, $`q^{(2)}`$ near $`k=0`$, i.e. $`\tilde P`$ near $`(\pi,\dots)`$) has the imaginary-time covariance $`\frac{T_0}{4\pi^2c}\frac1{r^2+c^2\tau^2}`$ with $`T_0=2zJS`$, $`c=JS\sqrt{2z}`$; covariances with momenta carry extra derivatives. Far from $`h`$ the push enters only through the zero modes, whose contribution vanishes as $`L\to\infty`$ (as $`N^{-1/2}`$ with (8.6)). `code/v5/spinwave_mutual.py` evaluates (8.15) on the lattice (exact $`T(k),V(k)`$, zero modes left out) on tori $`L=32,64,128`$, $`r=2,\dots,12`$ along an axis.

### Step 11. Relation source R2 (changed link)

$`\delta\,\mathbf S_x\cdot\mathbf S_y=-\delta S(S+1)+\frac{\delta S}2\sum_\alpha(q^{(\alpha)}_x-q^{(\alpha)}_y)^2`$: $`A=\delta S(e_x-e_y)(e_x-e_y)^{\rm T}`$, $`B=\delta S(e_x+e_y)(e_x+e_y)^{\rm T}`$, which vanish on the zero modes (unchanged by the common rotation). for the separation along an axis ($`\epsilon_i`$ is the class of the endpoint $`x`$ of link $`i`$, $`\mathbf e_i`$ points from $`y`$ to $`x`$; order $`\delta_1\delta_2S/J`$). ### Step 12. Relation source R1 (held spin)

(i) *Retarded vertex.* Conditions: $`\lvert g'\rvert\ll J`$ and $`\lvert g'\rvert s_b\ll JS`$; $`s_b`$ needs no scaling with $`S`$ beyond the second. With (8.5) and $`S^\alpha_b:=\mathbf e_\alpha\cdot\mathbf S_b`$,

```math
g'\mathbf S_b\cdot\mathbf S_x=g'\epsilon_x(S-n_x)\,\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf S_b+g'\sqrt S\,\epsilon_x\textstyle\sum_\alpha S^\alpha_b\,q^{(\alpha)}_x+O(g').
```

The first term splits the levels of $`b`$ by $`\Delta_b=\lvert g'\rvert S`$. The lowest level is $`\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf S_b=m_b:=\sigma\epsilon_x`$, with $`\sigma:=-s_b\,{\rm sgn}\,g'`$. In it, $`-g'\epsilon_xm_bn_x=\lvert g'\rvert s_bn_x`$ is the along-$`\mathbf n`$ vertex $`\frac12\lvert g'\rvert s_b\sum_\alpha(q^{(\alpha)}_x)^2`$ (Step 10 with $`g=-\epsilon_x\lvert g'\rvert s_b`$). Next, $`b`$ is eliminated at second order in the transverse term: this is the cumulant of the imaginary-time action, with $`b`$ in its lowest level and the field Gaussian (8.5). Only $`S^\mp_b`$ connects $`m_b`$ to the next level, with squared matrix element $`2s_b`$, so the time-ordered correlator of $`b`$ is $`\chi_{\alpha\beta}(\tau)=\frac{s_b}2e^{-\Delta_b\lvert\tau\rvert}[\delta_{\alpha\beta}+i\frac{m_b}{s_b}{\rm sgn}(\tau)\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta}]`$, with $`\varepsilon_{12}=-\varepsilon_{21}=1`$. Adding the along vertex, the field at $`x`$ receives the term $`\frac12\int\frac{d\nu}{2\pi}q_x^{\rm T}(-\nu)K(\nu)q_x(\nu)`$, where $`\nu`$ is the imaginary frequency and

```math
K(\nu)=\lvert g'\rvert s_b\,\frac{\nu^2}{\nu^2+\Delta_b^2}\,\mathbb 1+\lvert g'\rvert\sigma\epsilon_x\,\frac{\Delta_b\nu}{\nu^2+\Delta_b^2}\,\varepsilon .
```

$`K(0)=0`$: $`b`$ follows the orientation. This holds at every order, since a static tilt of the field near $`x`$ together with $`b`$ is a joint rotation, which leaves the R1 term unchanged. For $`\nu\ll\Delta_b`$ the antisymmetric part is $`\frac{\sigma\epsilon_x}S\nu\,\varepsilon`$, independent of $`g'`$. With $`q=\sqrt S\theta`$ it is the precession term $`\frac\sigma2(\theta_1\dot\theta_2-\theta_2\dot\theta_1)`$ of the spin $`\sigma`$ that follows the orientation. No multiplet of the pair $`b,x`$ is used: the exchange of $`x`$ with its neighbours is contained in the propagator of $`q_x`$. Three kinds of term are omitted. Higher orders in the transverse term are of relative order $`\langle(q^{(\alpha)}_x)^2\rangle/S=O(1/S)`$. The reaction of the field on $`b`$ is of relative order $`O(\lvert g'\rvert/(JS))`$, since its self-energy $`\sim g'^2S\,\hat g(0)/(JS)`$ is small against $`\Delta_b`$. Higher orders in each vertex are of relative order $`O(\lvert g'\rvert s_b\gamma_3/(JS))`$.

(ii) *Mutual cost.* With two such vertices, the second-order formula (8.15) becomes $`\mathcal E_{12}=-\frac12\int\frac{d\nu}{2\pi}{\rm Tr}[K_1G_{12}K_2G_{21}]`$, where $`G_{12}(\nu)`$ is the matrix of the $`\tau`$-transforms of $`\langle T\,q^{(\alpha)}_{x_1}(\tau)q^{(\beta)}_{x_2}(0)\rangle`$. At large $`r`$, $`G_{12}=G\,\mathbb 1`$ with $`G(\nu)=\frac{T_0}{4\pi c^2r}e^{-\lvert\nu\rvert r/c}`$, the transform of the covariance of Step 9. The off-diagonal part comes from the momenta in $`q^{(\alpha)}`$ and is treated below. For $`K_i=a_i\mathbb 1+b_i\varepsilon`$, $`{\rm Tr}(K_1K_2)=2(a_1a_2-b_1b_2)`$. With $`\int\frac{d\nu}{2\pi}G^2=\frac{\kappa_\parallel}{JSr^3}`$ (Step 10), $`u=2\lvert\nu\rvert r/c`$, $`\ell_b:=c/\Delta_b=\sqrt{2z}J/\lvert g'\rvert`$ and $`\beta_i:=2r/\ell_{b_i}`$:

```math
\mathcal E^{R1}_{12}=-\frac{\lvert g'_1g'_2\rvert\,\kappa_\parallel}{JS\,r^3}\,\mathcal F_{12},\qquad \mathcal F_{12}=\int_0^\infty du\,e^{-u}\,\frac{u^2\bigl(s_{b_1}s_{b_2}u^2-\sigma_1\sigma_2\epsilon_{x_1}\epsilon_{x_2}\beta_1\beta_2\bigr)}{(u^2+\beta_1^2)(u^2+\beta_2^2)}.\qquad\text{(8.18)}
```

The relative remainder of (8.18) is $`O(r^{-2})`$ (lattice), $`O(1/S)`$, $`O(\lvert g'\rvert/J)`$ and $`O(\lvert g'\rvert s_b/(JS))`$. *Off-diagonal part.* The continuum covariance is $`G_{12}=G(\mathbb 1+\gamma'\varepsilon)`$ with $`\gamma'=-(\epsilon_{x_1}+\epsilon_{x_2})\nu/T_0`$, and $`G_{21}=G_{12}`$. Matrices $`x\mathbb 1+y\varepsilon`$ multiply like complex numbers $`x+iy`$, so $`{\rm Tr}[K_1G_{12}K_2G_{21}]=2{\rm Re}[k_1k_2g^2]`$. With it the integrand of (8.18), $`a_1a_2-b_1b_2`$ in units of $`\lvert g'_1g'_2\rvert`$, becomes $`(a_1a_2-b_1b_2)(1-\gamma^2)+2\gamma(a_1b_2+a_2b_1)`$, where $`\gamma=(\epsilon_{x_1}+\epsilon_{x_2})cu/(2rT_0)`$. From $`K_i`$, $`b_i/a_i={\rm sgn}(\sigma_i)\,\epsilon_{x_i}\Delta_i/\nu`$ with $`\Delta_i=\lvert g'_i\rvert S`$, and $`\gamma\ne0`$ only for $`\epsilon_{x_1}=\epsilon_{x_2}`$. Hence the added term vanishes iff $`\epsilon_{x_1}\ne\epsilon_{x_2}`$ or $`{\rm sgn}(\sigma_1)\Delta_1+{\rm sgn}(\sigma_2)\Delta_2=0`$ (opposite signs and equal gaps). Otherwise it adds to $`\mathcal F_{12}`$

```math
\delta\mathcal F_{12}=\frac{4s_{b_1}s_{b_2}}{T_0}\bigl[{\rm sgn}(\sigma_1)\Delta_1+{\rm sgn}(\sigma_2)\Delta_2\bigr]\int_0^\infty du\,\frac{e^{-u}u^4}{(u^2+\beta_1^2)(u^2+\beta_2^2)} .
```

For $`r\ll\ell_b`$ the integral is $`1+O(\beta)`$, so $`\delta\mathcal F_{12}`$ is of relative order $`\lvert g'\rvert/J`$ against the scale $`s_{b_1}s_{b_2}`$ of $`\mathcal F_{12}`$. For $`r\gg\ell_b`$ it is $`24/(\beta_1\beta_2)^2`$, which gives the relative correction $`-\frac{12}{\sqrt{2z}}\,\frac{{\rm sgn}(\sigma_1)\ell_{b_1}+{\rm sgn}(\sigma_2)\ell_{b_2}}{r^2}`$ to the limit $`r\gg\ell_b`$ below; this is small against its remainder $`O(\ell_b^2/r^2)`$, since $`\ell_b\gg1`$. Both lie within the stated remainders. Near the zero $`r_0`$ of $`\mathcal F_{12}`$ (case $`\varsigma=+1`$ below) a relative remainder has no meaning. There the remainders are absolute: $`\mathcal F_{12}`$ is fixed up to $`s_{b_1}s_{b_2}[O(r^{-2})+O(1/S)+O(\lvert g'\rvert/J)+O(\lvert g'\rvert s_b/(JS))]`$, and $`r_0`$ up to a relative shift of the same order. Write $`\varsigma:={\rm sgn}(\sigma_1\sigma_2)\epsilon_{x_1}\epsilon_{x_2}`$. The integrals $`\int_0^\infty\frac{u^2du}{(u^2+\beta_1^2)(u^2+\beta_2^2)}=\frac\pi{2(\beta_1+\beta_2)}`$ and $`\int_0^\infty\frac{du}{(u^2+\beta_1^2)(u^2+\beta_2^2)}=\frac\pi{2\beta_1\beta_2(\beta_1+\beta_2)}`$, and $`\int_0^\infty u^{2n}e^{-u}du=(2n)!`$, give the two limits:

```math
\mathcal F_{12}=\begin{cases}s_{b_1}s_{b_2}\Bigl[1-\dfrac\pi2\,\dfrac{\beta_1^2+\beta_1\beta_2+\beta_2^2+\varsigma\beta_1\beta_2}{\beta_1+\beta_2}+O(\beta^2\ln\beta)\Bigr], & r\ll\ell_b,\\[2ex] -\dfrac{2\sigma_1\sigma_2\epsilon_{x_1}\epsilon_{x_2}}{\beta_1\beta_2}\Bigl[1-\dfrac{12}{\beta_1\beta_2}\Bigl(\varsigma+\dfrac{\beta_1}{\beta_2}+\dfrac{\beta_2}{\beta_1}\Bigr)+O(\beta^{-4})\Bigr], & r\gg\ell_b,\end{cases}
```

i.e. $`\mathcal E^{R1}_{12}=-\lvert g'_1g'_2\rvert s_{b_1}s_{b_2}\frac{\kappa_\parallel}{JSr^3}[1+O(r/\ell_b)]`$ for $`1\ll r\ll\ell_b`$ (order $`g'_1g'_2`$; the term of order $`r/\ell_b`$ includes the antisymmetric part, whose low-frequency region gives the term $`\varsigma\beta_1\beta_2`$), and

```math
\mathcal E^{R1}_{12}=\frac{3\sqrt3}{8\pi^3}\,\frac JS\,\frac{\sigma_1\sigma_2\epsilon_{x_1}\epsilon_{x_2}}{r^5}\bigl[1+O(\ell_b^2/r^2)\bigr]\quad(r\gg\ell_b),
```

independent of $`\lvert g'\rvert`$ (with $`\beta_1\beta_2=4r^2\lvert g'_1g'_2\rvert S^2/c^2`$ and $`c^2=2zJ^2S^2`$, the prefactor is $`z\kappa_\parallel=\frac{3\sqrt3}{8\pi^3}=0.020948`$). This is the law in the order of the question ($`r\to\infty`$ last at fixed $`g'`$); it is not analytic in $`g'`$, because its range $`r\gg\ell_b\propto1/\lvert g'\rvert`$ recedes as $`g'\to0`$. Sign: for $`\varsigma=-1`$ the integrand of (8.18) is positive, so the cost is negative at every $`r`$. For equal $`\ell_b`$ the integrand is $`u^2/(u^2+\beta^2)`$, which decreases in $`\beta`$, so the cost rises monotonically to zero: attractive at every $`r`$. For $`\varsigma=+1`$ it is attractive for $`r\ll\ell_b`$ and repulsive for $`r\gg\ell_b`$; for equal $`\ell_b`$, (8.18) changes sign at $`\beta_0=1.698`$, i.e. $`r_0=0.849\,\ell_b`$ (computed). No term of R1 is linear in $`q^{(\alpha)}`$: the lowest eigenvector can be taken as an eigenvector of the conserved $`\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf S_{\rm tot}`$, and in it $`\langle S^\alpha_b\rangle=0`$. So there is no $`1/r`$ part.

### Step 13. Without a push (item 3(c), short argument)

*Direction sources* (cubic torus, order $`L\to\infty`$ first, $`\lvert g_i\rvert\ll JS`$). Let $`\mathbf h_i:=c_ig_i\epsilon_{x_i}\mathbf m_i`$ ($`c_i=1`$ single object, $`2`$ cell), $`\mathbf h_{\rm eff}=\mathbf h_1+\mathbf h_2`$, and let $`\hat{\mathbf n}`$ be the orientation far from the sources (Step 3 with $`\mathbf n\to\hat{\mathbf n}`$, no push; $`\perp`$ is now relative to $`\hat{\mathbf n}`$). At fixed $`\hat{\mathbf n}`$ the along parts of the sources are anchors $`a_i=-\mathbf h_i\cdot\hat{\mathbf n}`$ with $`\lvert a_i\rvert\gamma_3\le\lvert\mathbf h_i\rvert\gamma_3\ll JS`$. A negative anchor binds a mode of $`JS\Delta+aP_x`$ on the infinite cubic lattice only for $`a\le-JS/\gamma_3`$ (rank-one criterion), so at fixed $`\hat{\mathbf n}`$ the deviations stay small, and the anchors change the response by relative $`O(g/(JS))`$. The transverse parts are linear sources $`\sqrt S\,g_i\epsilon_{x_i}\mathbf m_i^\perp`$ on each object of source $`i`$. The deviations vanish far away, and the shift of Step 6 with $`(JS\Delta)^+`$ ($`\hat g_L\to\hat g_\infty`$) gives the cost of the orientation. With $`y_i:=\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\mathbf h_i`$ and $`\mathbf h_i^\perp\cdot\mathbf h_j^\perp=\mathbf h_i\cdot\mathbf h_j-y_iy_j`$,

```math
E(\hat{\mathbf n})=S\,\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\mathbf h_{\rm eff}-\frac1{2J}\sum_{i,j=1,2}w_{ij}\,\mathbf h_i^\perp\cdot\mathbf h_j^\perp=S(y_1+y_2)+\frac1{2J}\,y^{\rm T}wy-\frac1{2J}\sum_{i,j}w_{ij}\,\mathbf h_i\cdot\mathbf h_j,\qquad w_{ij}=\frac1{c_ic_j}\sum_{a\in i,\,b\in j}\hat g_\infty(a-b),
```

with a relative remainder $`O(g/(JS))`$ in the quadratic part, $`w_{ii}=\gamma_3`$ for a single object and $`w_{12}=\frac1{4\pi r}+O(r^{-3})`$ (Step 8). The matrix $`w`$ is positive definite, since $`\hat g_\infty`$ is a positive kernel and the two sources have different supports. The lowest eigenvector takes the minimizing $`\hat{\mathbf n}`$ (the sources pin the zero modes as the push does in (8.6); rotor levels of order $`J/N`$ aside). By the envelope theorem $`\partial E_{\min}/\partial w_{12}=-\mathbf h_1^\perp\cdot\mathbf h_2^\perp/J`$ at the minimizing $`\hat{\mathbf n}`$. So the separation part is $`-\mathbf h_1^\perp\cdot\mathbf h_2^\perp/(4\pi Jr)+O(r^{-2})`$ there. This is the form of (8.14), where the push fixes $`\hat{\mathbf n}=\mathbf n`$.

*The shared orientation.* $`E`$ is strictly convex in $`y`$. Its unconstrained minimum $`y^\circ=-JS\,w^{-1}(1,1)^{\rm T}`$ has $`\lvert y^\circ_i\rvert\simeq JS/w_{ii}\gg\lvert\mathbf h_i\rvert`$ ($`w_{12}\to0`$ at large $`r`$). One source alone has $`y_1\in[-\lvert\mathbf h_1\rvert,\lvert\mathbf h_1\rvert]`$; its minimum is at $`y_1=-\lvert\mathbf h_1\rvert`$, i.e. $`\hat{\mathbf n}=-\hat{\mathbf h}_1`$ and $`\mathbf h_1^\perp=0`$, with $`E_1=-S\lvert\mathbf h_1\rvert`$. For two sources, $`E_{\min}\le E(-\hat{\mathbf h}_{\rm eff})`$ and $`E\ge S(y_1+y_2)-\frac1{2J}\sum w_{ij}\mathbf h_i\cdot\mathbf h_j`$ give $`E_{\min}=-S\lvert\mathbf h_{\rm eff}\rvert+O(\gamma_3\lvert\mathbf h\rvert^2/J)`$, with $`\lvert\mathbf h\rvert:=\max_i\lvert\mathbf h_i\rvert`$. The part of the lowest eigenvalue that depends on both sources but not on $`r`$ is therefore $`\mathcal E^{(0)}_{12}=E_{\min}-E_1-E_2`$ at $`r\to\infty`$, given in (8.19). It is homogeneous of degree one in the strengths and not analytic at zero strength, so it has no expansion in them. At this order it is non-negative (triangle inequality), and zero only for $`\mathbf h_1,\mathbf h_2`$ in the same direction. With the push its place is taken by the bilinear constant $`-\epsilon_{x_1}\epsilon_{x_2}g_1g_2(\mathbf m_1^\perp\cdot\mathbf m_2^\perp)c_{\rm cell}S/h_p`$ of (8.14), from the common rotation against the push. Without a push, the mutual cost at the lowest order in the strengths is this constant. Its separation dependence starts at order $`\lvert\mathbf h\rvert^2/J`$, with the separation part above.

(α) *Non-parallel sources* ($`\mathbf m_1\nparallel\mathbf m_2`$), every ratio of the strengths. Write $`\hat{\mathbf n}=\hat{\mathbf n}_P+t\,\mathbf v_0`$ with $`\hat{\mathbf n}_P`$ in the plane $`P`$ of $`\mathbf m_1,\mathbf m_2`$ and $`\mathbf v_0\perp P`$. Then $`y`$ depends only on $`\hat{\mathbf n}_P`$, and $`\lvert\hat{\mathbf n}_P\rvert\le1`$. So $`y`$ ranges over a filled ellipse $`\mathcal D\subset\{\lvert y_i\rvert\le\lvert\mathbf h_i\rvert\}`$, whose boundary is the set of orientations in $`P`$; each interior point is a mirror pair $`\pm t`$. A strictly convex function on a compact convex set whose unconstrained minimum lies outside it has a unique minimizer, on the boundary. Hence the orientation is unique and lies in $`P`$. Let $`\mathbf t\in P`$ be the unit vector $`\perp\hat{\mathbf n}`$; then $`\mathbf h_i^\perp=\tau_i\mathbf t`$ with $`\tau_i:=\mathbf h_i\cdot\mathbf t`$. Stationarity along the boundary gives $`\tau_1\partial_{y_1}E+\tau_2\partial_{y_2}E=0`$ with $`\partial_{y_i}E=S+(wy)_i/J=S[1+O(\gamma_3\lvert\mathbf h\rvert/(JS))]>0`$. So $`\tau_1\tau_2<0`$ (both zero would make $`\mathbf m_1\parallel\mathbf m_2`$), and the separation part $`-\tau_1\tau_2/(4\pi Jr)=+\tau_1^2/(4\pi Jr)[1+O(\gamma_3\lvert\mathbf h\rvert/(JS))]`$ is repulsive. For non-cancelling sources, $`SJ\lvert\mathbf h_{\rm eff}\rvert\gg\gamma_3\lvert\mathbf h\rvert^2`$, the linear term dominates. Then $`\hat{\mathbf n}=-\hat{\mathbf h}_{\rm eff}`$ up to an angle $`O(\gamma_3\lvert\mathbf h\rvert^2/(SJ\lvert\mathbf h_{\rm eff}\rvert))`$, and $`\tau_1^2=\lvert\mathbf h_1^\perp\rvert^2`$ taken relative to $`\hat{\mathbf h}_{\rm eff}`$. In the corner $`SJ\lvert\mathbf h_{\rm eff}\rvert\lesssim\gamma_3\lvert\mathbf h\rvert^2`$ (nearly opposite $`\mathbf h_i`$), $`\hat{\mathbf n}`$ is the minimum of $`E`$ on the unit circle of $`P`$. This one-variable problem is not solved here in closed form; uniqueness, the plane and the sign hold there as well. These conclusions use only three facts. First, $`E`$ depends on $`\hat{\mathbf n}`$ only through $`y`$. This is exact: $`E`$ is unchanged by a common rotation of $`\hat{\mathbf n},\mathbf h_1,\mathbf h_2`$ and by $`(\hat{\mathbf n},\mathbf h_i)\to(-\hat{\mathbf n},-\mathbf h_i)`$ (the antiunitary $`\mathbf S\to-\mathbf S`$), so the triple product $`\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot(\mathbf h_1\times\mathbf h_2)`$ enters only through its square, a function of $`y`$. Second, $`E`$ is strictly convex. Third, its gradient is $`\simeq S(1,1)`$. The remainders $`O(g/(JS))`$ and $`O(1/S)`$ do not change these.

(β) *Parallel* $`\mathbf m_1\parallel\mathbf m_2`$, every ratio of the strengths. Here $`\mathcal D`$ degenerates to the segment $`y=(h_1,h_2)\,\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\mathbf m`$, whose interior points are circles about $`\mathbf m`$, and the minimum of $`E`$ on its line can lie inside. With $`\mathbf h_i=h_i\mathbf m`$, $`H:=\lvert h_1+h_2\rvert`$, $`u:=\lvert\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\mathbf m\rvert`$. Then $`E=-SHu-\frac Q{2J}(1-u^2)`$ with $`Q=w_{11}h_1^2+w_{22}h_2^2+2w_{12}h_1h_2>0`$, minimal at $`u^*=\min(1,SJH/Q)`$; define $`H_c:=Q/(SJ)`$. For $`H\ge H_c`$ the orientation is $`-\hat{\mathbf h}_{\rm eff}`$, there is no $`1/r`$ part, and (8.16) applies. For $`H<H_c`$ it cants: it lies on the cone $`u=u^*`$ about $`\mathbf m`$ and is fixed only up to rotations about $`\mathbf m`$. At $`u=1`$, (5.6) reads $`H/r_{12}+h_1h_2>0`$, i.e. $`H>H_c`$ up to relative $`O(g/(JS))`$, so the canted range is exactly the range in which $`-\hat{\mathbf h}_{\rm eff}`$ is unstable. $`H<H_c`$ needs $`h_1h_2<0`$: for $`h_1h_2>0`$, $`H_c\le\gamma_3H^2/(SJ)\ll H`$. So

```math
\begin{aligned}\text{no push:}\quad&\mathcal E^{(0)}_{12}=S\bigl(\lvert\mathbf h_1\rvert+\lvert\mathbf h_2\rvert-\lvert\mathbf h_{\rm eff}\rvert\bigr)+O\Bigl(\frac{\gamma_3\lvert\mathbf h\rvert^2}J\Bigr);\qquad \mathcal E_{12}\big|_{\mathbf r}=-\frac{\mathbf h_1^\perp\cdot\mathbf h_2^\perp}{4\pi Jr}\Big|_{\hat{\mathbf n}=\arg\min E}+O(r^{-2});\\&\text{(α): }+\frac{\tau_1^2}{4\pi Jr}\Bigl[1+O\Bigl(\frac{\gamma_3\lvert\mathbf h\rvert}{JS}\Bigr)\Bigr]>0;\qquad\text{(β): }-\frac{h_1h_2\,(1-u^{*2})}{4\pi Jr},\ u^*=\min\Bigl(1,\frac{SJH}Q\Bigr);\qquad\text{relation sources fix no orientation.}\end{aligned}\qquad\text{(8.19)}
```

In (β) the separation part is repulsive for $`H<H_c`$, equals $`+h_1^2/(4\pi Jr)`$ at $`\mathbf h_{\rm eff}=0`$, and vanishes for $`H\ge H_c`$.

*Relation sources.* R2 ($`J+\delta>0`$) keeps the bipartite antiferromagnetic structure; R1 too, with $`b`$ in the class $`-\epsilon_x`$ for $`g'>0`$ and $`\epsilon_x`$ (ferromagnetic within a class) for $`g'<0`$. By Lieb–Mattis (which allows ferromagnetic terms within a class) the lowest eigenvalue has total spin $`\lvert\sum_i\epsilon_{x_i}\sigma_i\rvert`$ (field and held objects) and is $`(2S_{\rm tot}+1)`$-fold degenerate; for $`S_{\rm tot}=0`$ the lowest eigenvector is unique, unchanged by every common rotation, with $`\langle\mathbf S_x\rangle=0`$. Relation sources therefore fix no orientation. Their vertices are unchanged by the common rotation, so without a push the separation dependence (8.17), (8.18) is unchanged as $`L\to\infty`$: only rotor levels of order $`J/N`$ differ.

## Result

All results: exchange cells, cubic torus or ring of even side $`L\ge4`$, the setting of the question; $`\epsilon_h=+1`$. **Assumption E** (Setup): the lowest eigenvector of the expansion (8.5), truncated at quadratic order, is taken to give the quantities of the true lowest eigenvector asymptotically in $`1/S`$ while every $`\langle n_x\rangle\ll S`$. The results that rest on it say so. (8.1)–(8.4) and the parts of (8.12) and (8.19) marked "Exact" do not rest on it; (8.9) is numerical.

- **(8.1)** $`J>0`$, field alone, every $`S`$, both graphs: the lowest eigenvector is unique, a singlet, unchanged by every common rotation, $`\langle\mathbf S_x\rangle=0`$.
- **(8.2)** $`J<0`$, field alone: lowest eigenvalue $`JS^2DN`$, $`(2NS+1)`$-fold degenerate; no lowest eigenvector is unchanged by all common rotations.
- **(8.3)** $`J<0`$ with push: unique lowest eigenvector, the product state; $`\langle\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf S_x\rangle=S`$ exactly for every $`x`$, $`L`$, $`S`$, $`h_p>0`$, both graphs and both orders of limits.
- **(8.4)** $`J>0`$, order $`h_p\to0`$ at fixed $`L`$, then $`L\to\infty`$: $`m(x)=O(h_p)\to0`$, every $`S`$, both graphs (exact).
- **(8.5)** The expansion on which Assumption E rests: at leading order in $`1/S`$ about the orientation set by the push, $`C=E_0+\frac12\sum_{\alpha=1,2}q^{(\alpha){\rm T}}(JS\Delta+h_pP_h)q^{(\alpha)}+O(J)`$, $`q^{(\alpha)}_x=\epsilon_x\mathbf e_\alpha\cdot\mathbf S_x/\sqrt S`$, $`[q^{(1)}_x,q^{(2)}_y]=i\epsilon_x\delta_{xy}`$; the quartic remainder is of relative order $`1/S`$ while $`\langle n_x\rangle\ll S`$.
- **(8.6)** Under Assumption E (its condition $`\langle n_x\rangle\ll S`$ with the push): $`Nh_{\rm eff}S\gg zJ`$ is required. Here $`h_{\rm eff}=(1/h_p+\hat g_L(0)/(JS))^{-1}`$ is the restoring coefficient of the collective rotation, lowered from $`h_p`$ by the relaxation of the field near the push; on the cubic torus as $`L\to\infty`$, $`h_{\rm eff}=h_p/(1+\eta\gamma_3)`$. Equivalently, $`Nh_pS\gg zJ`$ and $`NS^2\gg z\hat g_L(0)`$. The condition is met in the order $`L\to\infty`$ first (on the ring if $`S^2\gg\frac16`$) and violated in the order $`h_p\to0`$ at fixed $`L`$.
- **(8.7)** Under Assumption E, cubic torus, $`J>0`$, order $`L\to\infty`$, $`R\to\infty`$, then $`h_p\to0`$: $`m(x)\to\epsilon_h[S-\Delta S_3+O(1/S)]`$, $`\Delta S_3=0.0784`$, independent of $`h_p>0`$. The expansion is taken at fixed $`L`$ and fixed $`\eta=h_p/(JS)`$ under (8.6), and its coefficients have uniform limits as $`L\to\infty`$. At fixed $`\eta`$ and $`L`$ the collective rotation adds $`\frac12\sqrt{2zJS/(Nh_{\rm eff})}`$ to $`\Delta S`$ at the far place. At fixed $`h_p`$ and fixed $`L`$ only $`m(x)=S-\frac12\sqrt{2zJS/(Nh_p)}+O(S^0)`$ is stated: the collective rotation contributes at order $`S^{1/2}`$ (there $`h_{\rm eff}=h_p[1+O(1/S)]`$), and its quartic terms, of order $`zJ/(Nh_p)`$, are estimated. Both vanish as $`L\to\infty`$, so the limit holds in either scaling.
- **(8.8)** Under Assumption E, ring (control): $`m(R)=S-\frac1\pi\ln R+O(1)+O(1/S)`$; the first correction is not uniform in $`R`$, and the expansion in $`1/S`$ does not decide the order $`L\to\infty`$, $`R\to\infty`$ at any $`S`$.
- **(8.9)** Exact diagonalization ($`S=\frac12`$: rings $`L\le20`$ and one cubic cluster of 16 objects; $`S=1`$: rings $`L\le12`$), with every sector of $`\sum_xS^3_x`$ searched, at the sampled $`h_p/J=0.01,0.1,0.3,1,3,10`$. The lowest eigenvector is unique and lies in $`M=0`$. At fixed size $`m_{\rm far}`$ is linear in $`h_p`$ at the two smallest strengths. On rings with $`h_p\ge J`$ it decreases with $`L`$ over the computed sizes (values in (8.9)). For $`S=\frac12`$ on the cubic torus the order $`L\to\infty`$, $`R\to\infty`$ is undecided at these sizes.
- **(8.10)** Under Assumption E, identification at leading order in $`1/S`$: two conjugate copies $`q^{(\alpha)}`$ with the 05-anchor matrix $`A=V_a`$, $`\kappa_h=JS`$, $`\kappa_a=h_p`$, $`g_s=\epsilon_{x_s}\sqrt Sg(\mathbf m\cdot\mathbf e_\alpha)`$, exact for all static results of the quadratic expansion; kinetic matrix $`EV_aE`$ instead of $`\mathbb 1`$. Separately, as an approximation for dynamics, the long-wavelength model: two copies of the 05-anchor field with $`\frac12p^2`$, $`\kappa_h=2zJ^2S^2`$, $`g_s=\epsilon_{x_s}S\sqrt{2zJ}g(\mathbf m\cdot\mathbf e_\alpha)`$ and the anchor $`\kappa_a=2zJSh_p`$, without local momentum terms. The anchor is matched to the exact static result: the static results of the model with linear sources (source-dependent lowest eigenvalue, $`\langle q\rangle`$) equal those of (8.5) exactly, for every $`\eta`$ and $`L`$. The local momentum terms that the long-wavelength decomposition gives at $`h`$ would change the static anchor to $`\kappa_a/(1+\eta/(2z))`$; they double count the staggered part of $`\tilde Q_h`$ and are not used. For dynamics, on the cubic torus in the order $`L\to\infty`$ first, the model holds at wave numbers $`k\ll1`$ for every $`\eta`$. Its local error at $`h`$ is of relative order $`k`$, odd in frequency and coupling the copies, and its bulk error is of relative order $`k^2`$. Only as $`k\to0`$ are the copies independent ($`T_0=2zJS`$). On the ring only the static statements of the model are derived.
- **(8.11)** Under Assumption E, conditions of (8.10): $`\Delta S_3\ll S`$, $`Nh_{\rm eff}S\gg zJ`$ (8.6), $`\lvert g\rvert\ll h_p`$, $`\lvert g\rvert\ll JS`$; on the ring also $`\frac1\pi\ln d\ll S`$ and $`\lvert g\rvert d\ll JS`$ at the distances used.
- **(8.12)** Under (8.10), (8.11) and Assumption E: (5.3) with the anchor, (5.8), (5.10), (5.11), (5.14), (5.15) hold, and "$`-\infty`$" becomes a finite rotation; the uniform shift of (5.11) is the small-angle form of a bounded rotation; at the next order in $`1/S`$ the coefficients change by relative $`O(1/S)`$. Exact (A1, (8.1)): the outcome "no lowest eigenvector" of (5.3)–(5.6) fails, because a lowest eigenvector exists.
- **(8.13)** Under Assumption E, transverse direction sources, order $`g_1g_2`$, leading order in $`1/S`$, every $`L`$, both graphs: $`\mathcal E^\perp_{12}=-S\epsilon_{x_1}\epsilon_{x_2}g_1g_2(\mathbf m_1^\perp\cdot\mathbf m_2^\perp)G_a(x_1,x_2)`$.
- **(8.14)** Under Assumption E, cubic torus, order $`L\to\infty`$, distances to $`h\to\infty`$, then $`r\to\infty`$: separation part $`-\epsilon_{x_1}\epsilon_{x_2}g_1g_2(\mathbf m_1^\perp\cdot\mathbf m_2^\perp)c_{\rm cell}/(4\pi J r)`$, with $`c_{\rm cell}=c_1c_2`$, where $`c_i=1`$ for a single object and $`2`$ for a two-object cell (so $`1`$, $`2`$ or $`4`$), $`r`$ the midpoint separation, remainder $`O(r^{-3})`$; long range, order $`S^0`$; it vanishes for a source at $`h`$ or for $`\mathbf m_1^\perp\cdot\mathbf m_2^\perp=0`$.
- **(8.16)** Under Assumption E, along-$`\mathbf n`$ direction sources: $`-\epsilon_{x_1}\epsilon_{x_2}g_1g_2(\mathbf m_1\cdot\mathbf n)(\mathbf m_2\cdot\mathbf n)c_{\rm cell}\kappa_\parallel/(JSr^3)[1+O(r^{-2})]`$ ($`r`$ the midpoint separation), $`c_{\rm cell}=c_1c_2`$ as in (8.14), $`\kappa_\parallel=\sqrt3/(16\pi^3)`$, order $`g_1g_2/(JS)`$; no long-range mark.
- **(8.17)** Under Assumption E, R2, separation along an axis: $`-\frac{\sqrt3}{8\pi^3}\frac{\delta_1\delta_2S}{Jr^7}a`$, order $`\delta_1\delta_2S/J`$. Here $`\epsilon_i`$ is the class of the endpoint $`x`$ of link $`i`$ and $`\mathbf e_i`$ points from $`y`$ to $`x`$; the result does not depend on which endpoint is called $`x`$. $`a=\frac{43}6+\frac52\epsilon_1\epsilon_2(\mathbf e_1\cdot\mathbf e_2)`$ for both links $`\parallel\mathbf r`$, $`\frac43-\frac5{12}\epsilon_1\epsilon_2(\mathbf e_1\cdot\mathbf e_2)`$ for both $`\perp\mathbf r`$ and parallel to each other, $`\frac1{12}`$ for both $`\perp\mathbf r`$ and $`\perp`$ each other, and $`-\frac12`$ for one link $`\parallel\mathbf r`$ and one $`\perp\mathbf r`$. For $`\delta_1\delta_2>0`$ the cost is attractive in the first three cases and repulsive in the last. No pair of orientations has a vanishing coefficient. The remainder is relative $`O(r^{-2})`$ at the midpoint separation, and $`O(r^{-1})`$ for the last two cases if the axis value is used. No long-range mark.
- **(8.18)** Under Assumption E, R1, cubic torus, order of item 3, at leading order in $`1/S`$, $`\lvert g'_i\rvert/J`$ and $`\lvert g'_i\rvert s_{b_i}/(JS)`$ (no other condition on $`s_b`$): $`\mathcal E^{R1}_{12}=-\frac{\lvert g'_1g'_2\rvert\kappa_\parallel}{JSr^3}\mathcal F_{12}`$, with $`\mathcal F_{12}`$ the integral of (8.18) in $`\beta_i=2r/\ell_{b_i}`$, $`\ell_b=\sqrt{2z}J/\lvert g'\rvert`$, $`\sigma_i=-s_{b_i}{\rm sgn}\,g'_i`$, and relative remainder $`O(r^{-2})`$. The momentum part of the propagators adds $`\delta\mathcal F_{12}`$ of Step 12(ii). It vanishes iff $`\epsilon_{x_1}\ne\epsilon_{x_2}`$ or $`{\rm sgn}(\sigma_1)\Delta_1+{\rm sgn}(\sigma_2)\Delta_2=0`$ ($`\Delta_i=\lvert g'_i\rvert S`$), and otherwise lies within the remainders. For $`1\ll r\ll\ell_b`$ it is $`-\lvert g'_1g'_2\rvert s_{b_1}s_{b_2}\kappa_\parallel/(JSr^3)[1+O(r/\ell_b)]`$, of order $`g'_1g'_2`$, with the term of order $`r/\ell_b`$ given in Step 12. For $`r\gg\ell_b`$, the law for $`r\to\infty`$ last, it is $`\frac{3\sqrt3}{8\pi^3}\frac JS\sigma_1\sigma_2\epsilon_{x_1}\epsilon_{x_2}r^{-5}[1+O(\ell_b^2/r^2)]`$, of order $`g'^0`$. Sign: for $`\sigma_1\sigma_2\epsilon_{x_1}\epsilon_{x_2}<0`$ it is negative at every $`r`$, and for equal $`\ell_b`$ attractive at every $`r`$ (rising monotonically to zero). For $`\sigma_1\sigma_2\epsilon_{x_1}\epsilon_{x_2}>0`$ it is attractive for $`r\ll\ell_b`$ and repulsive for $`r\gg\ell_b`$, changing sign at $`r_0=0.849\,\ell_b`$ for equal $`\ell_b`$. Near $`r_0`$ the remainders are absolute, $`s_{b_1}s_{b_2}[O(r^{-2})+O(1/S)+O(\lvert g'\rvert/J)+O(\lvert g'\rvert s_b/(JS))]`$, and fix $`r_0`$ up to a relative shift of that order. No long-range mark (no $`1/r`$ part).
- **(8.19)** Without a push. Under Assumption E, cubic torus, order $`L\to\infty`$ first, $`\lvert g_i\rvert\ll JS`$, with $`\mathbf h_i=c_ig_i\epsilon_{x_i}\mathbf m_i`$, $`\mathbf h_{\rm eff}=\mathbf h_1+\mathbf h_2`$ and $`\lvert\mathbf h\rvert=\max_i\lvert\mathbf h_i\rvert`$: the orientation $`\hat{\mathbf n}`$ minimizes $`E(\hat{\mathbf n})`$ of Step 13, which has a relative remainder $`O(g/(JS))`$ in its quadratic part. The part of the lowest eigenvalue that depends on both sources has a separation-independent part from the shared orientation, $`\mathcal E^{(0)}_{12}=S(\lvert\mathbf h_1\rvert+\lvert\mathbf h_2\rvert-\lvert\mathbf h_{\rm eff}\rvert)+O(\gamma_3\lvert\mathbf h\rvert^2/J)`$. It is of degree one in the strengths and not analytic at zero strength; at this order it is non-negative, and zero only for $`\mathbf h_1,\mathbf h_2`$ in the same direction. The separation dependence starts at order $`\lvert\mathbf h\rvert^2/J`$: it is $`-\mathbf h_1^\perp\cdot\mathbf h_2^\perp/(4\pi Jr)+O(r^{-2})`$ at the minimizing $`\hat{\mathbf n}`$. For $`\mathbf m_1\nparallel\mathbf m_2`$, at every ratio of the strengths, the orientation is unique and lies in the plane of $`\mathbf m_1,\mathbf m_2`$. The separation part is then $`+\tau_1^2/(4\pi Jr)[1+O(\gamma_3\lvert\mathbf h\rvert/(JS))]`$, with $`\tau_1\ne0`$ the component of $`\mathbf h_1`$ in that plane perpendicular to $`\hat{\mathbf n}`$: repulsive. For non-cancelling sources, $`SJ\lvert\mathbf h_{\rm eff}\rvert\gg\gamma_3\lvert\mathbf h\rvert^2`$, $`\hat{\mathbf n}=-\hat{\mathbf h}_{\rm eff}`$ up to an angle $`O(\gamma_3\lvert\mathbf h\rvert^2/(SJ\lvert\mathbf h_{\rm eff}\rvert))`$, and $`\tau_1^2=\lvert\mathbf h_1^\perp\rvert^2`$ relative to $`\hat{\mathbf h}_{\rm eff}`$. In the corner $`SJ\lvert\mathbf h_{\rm eff}\rvert\lesssim\gamma_3\lvert\mathbf h\rvert^2`$, $`\hat{\mathbf n}`$ is the minimum of $`E`$ on the unit circle of that plane, not given in closed form. For $`\mathbf m_1\parallel\mathbf m_2`$ it is $`-h_1h_2(1-u^{*2})/(4\pi Jr)`$ with $`u^*=\min(1,SJH/Q)`$. This vanishes for $`H\ge H_c=Q/(SJ)`$, where the orientation is $`-\hat{\mathbf h}_{\rm eff}`$ and (8.16) applies. For $`H<H_c`$, which needs $`h_1h_2<0`$, it is repulsive; the orientation cants and is fixed only up to rotations about $`\mathbf m`$, and the part equals $`+h_1^2/(4\pi Jr)`$ at $`\mathbf h_{\rm eff}=0`$. (8.17) and (8.18) are unchanged as $`L\to\infty`$. Exact (Lieb–Mattis): relation sources fix no orientation; the lowest eigenvalue is a multiplet of total spin $`\lvert\sum_i\epsilon_{x_i}\sigma_i\rvert`$, a unique singlet with $`\langle\mathbf S_x\rangle=0`$ when this is $`0`$, always for R2.

Controls: for (8.7) the ring (8.8), the order (8.4) and $`J<0`$ (8.3); for the long range of (8.14) the along-$`\mathbf n`$ sources (8.16), the relation sources (8.17), (8.18), and parallel sources without push with $`H\ge H_c`$ (8.19).

## Consistency checks

1. **$`S\to\infty`$ of item 1(b).** (8.7) gives $`m/S\to1`$: the product state with the staggered orientation $`\epsilon_x\mathbf n`$; for $`J<0`$ the product state (8.3) is exact at every $`S`$. Passed.
2. **Identification against (5.15).** With $`\kappa_h=JS`$, $`g_s^2=Sg^2`$ the coefficient $`g_{s1}g_{s2}/(4\pi\kappa_h)=g_1g_2/(4\pi J)`$ of (5.15) equals the lattice limit: $`4\pi r\hat g_\infty(r)\to1`$ (Step 8). In (8.17), the sum of the vertex over the three link directions is $`T_0[(\nabla\chi)^2+(\partial_{t'}\chi)^2]`$, the energy density of the copy (a uniform change of $`J`$), as it must be. Passed.
3. **Dimensions.** $`\mathcal E^\perp\sim g^2/J`$, $`\mathcal E^\parallel\sim g^2/(JS)`$, $`\mathcal E^{R1}\sim J/S`$ or $`g'^2/(JS)`$, $`\mathcal E^{R2}\sim\delta^2S/J`$: energies, and each scales as (vertex)$`^2`$/(level spacing $`JS`$). Passed.

## Open issues

- For $`S=\frac12`$ on the cubic torus the persistence of the orientation (order $`L\to\infty`$, $`R\to\infty`$) is undecided: one cluster of 16 objects.
- On the ring the methods used here do not decide $`m(x)`$ far from the push; exact diagonalization only shows a decrease with $`L`$ for $`L\le20`$.
- The next order in $`1/S`$ of (8.13)–(8.18) is not computed; that the $`1/r`$ law of (8.14) survives at all orders is not proven.
- The corrections near the push to (8.16)–(8.18) are not computed. (8.17) is classified only for separations along an axis; for other directions it is the stated integral.
- Without a push, for non-parallel sources near cancellation the orientation is the minimum of a one-variable function (Step 13), not given in closed form; its uniqueness, its plane and the repulsive sign of the separation part are derived.
- On the ring the dynamical range of the long-wavelength model (Step 6) is not derived; only its static statements are.
- The rotor regime $`Nh_{\rm eff}S\sim zJ`$ between the two orders of item 1(b) is not computed. At fixed $`L`$ and fixed $`h_p`$ the remainder of order $`S^0`$ of the collective rotation is estimated, not computed.
- The local terms at the push of the dynamical dictionary beyond the static matching (relative order $`k`$, odd in frequency) are bounded but not computed.

## Methods used

- Marshall–Lieb–Mattis and Lieb–Mattis theorems; Perron–Frobenius; analytic perturbation theory
- Holstein–Primakoff representation and expansion in $`1/S`$; quadratic forms, normal modes, Gaussian lowest eigenvectors
- Lattice Green functions on tori and their asymptotics; Wick's theorem; imaginary-time second-order perturbation theory
- Elimination of a held spin at second order (retarded vertex, imaginary frequency); dimension counting of local vertices
- Cost of the orientation as a collective coordinate; envelope theorem; positivity of a Kron-reduced form
- Matching of local coefficients to an exact static result; one-site T-matrix (Woodbury identity)
- Exact diagonalization (Lanczos) in all sectors of $`\sum_xS^3_x`$; Perron–Frobenius per sector
