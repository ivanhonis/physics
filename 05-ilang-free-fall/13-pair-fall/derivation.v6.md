## Derivation

### Step 1. Reduced contract (item 1a)

1. Every term of $`C`$ is the identity on $`\mathcal H_f`$, or $`\theta_i\,\hat x_i\otimes G_i\otimes\Phi`$, or $`C_f`$. With $`\Phi\varphi=\gamma\varphi`$ and $`C_f\varphi=e_f\varphi`$, for every $`\Psi\in\mathcal H_{b_1}\otimes\mathcal H_{b_2}\otimes\mathcal H_c`$ (the computation of (2.3) and (8.10); $`U`$ is carried along unchanged):

```math
C\,(\Psi\otimes\varphi)=\bigl((C_{\rm red}+e_f)\Psi\bigr)\otimes\varphi,\qquad C_{\rm red}=\sum_{i=1,2}\Bigl[A_i+\sum_xP^{(i)}_x\otimes(1+g_ix)\,G_i+\kappa\,\hat x_i\otimes X\Bigr]+U .
\qquad\text{(13.1)}
```

2. Hence $`e^{-iC\lambda}(\Psi\otimes\varphi)=e^{-ie_f\lambda}\,(e^{-iC_{\rm red}\lambda}\Psi)\otimes\varphi`$ for all $`\lambda`$: the view of $`\{b_1,b_2,c\}`$ is pure and evolves by $`C_{\rm red}`$ (exact; derived for this start of the field, and not claimed for another one).
3. $`C_{\rm red}`$ is the contract of an admissible description of $`b_1,b_2,c`$. It is self-adjoint and independent of $`\lambda`$; $`U`$ is a pair term of $`b_1,b_2`$; $`\kappa\,\hat x_i\otimes X`$ are pair terms of $`b_i,c`$; $`A_i+\sum_xP^{(i)}_x\otimes(1+g_ix)G_i`$ acts on one object and is included in a pair term (A5). Its position-dependent rest and clock term is then a property of the type of $`b_i`$ (A10).
4. A binding with a factor of the field is a term $`U'\otimes\Phi'`$ with $`U'`$ self-adjoint on $`\mathcal H_{b_1}\otimes\mathcal H_{b_2}`$ and $`\Phi'`$ self-adjoint on $`\mathcal H_f`$, or a sum of such terms, for example $`\sum_{x_1,x_2}\lvert x_1\rangle\langle x_1\rvert\otimes\lvert x_2\rangle\langle x_2\rvert\otimes W(x_1-x_2)\otimes\Phi_{x_1x_2}`$. Decompose the operators of each object into $`\mathbb C\mathbb 1`$ and the traceless ones. A sum of pair terms has no component that is traceless on the three objects $`b_1`$, $`b_2`$, $`f`$ at once (a pair term is the identity on all objects but two), and an operator on $`\mathcal H_{b_1}\otimes\mathcal H_{b_2}\otimes\mathcal H_f`$ without such a component is a sum of pair terms (each of its other components is the identity on at least one of the three). The component of $`U'\otimes\Phi'`$ that is traceless on all three is the product of the part of $`U'`$ that is traceless on both bodies and the traceless part of $`\Phi'`$. Hence $`U'\otimes\Phi'`$ is not a sum of pair terms, and so not of the form (1.10), iff $`U'\neq u_1\otimes\mathbb 1+\mathbb 1\otimes u_2`$ and $`\Phi'\notin\mathbb C\mathbb 1`$. Under these two conditions, and under them only, a binding with a factor of the field needs a term on three objects, which A5 does not contain. The two cases without the obstruction:
   - Scalar case, $`\Phi'=c\,\mathbb 1_f`$: the term is $`c\,U'`$, an admissible pair term of $`b_1`$, $`b_2`$. The binding then carries a number, not an operator of the field.
   - Reducible case, $`U'=u_1\otimes\mathbb 1+\mathbb 1\otimes u_2`$: the term is $`u_1\otimes\Phi'+u_2\otimes\Phi'`$, two admissible pair terms of a body and the field. It couples each body to the field, as the lapse-type terms of $`C`$ do, and binds nothing. A non-zero binding of the class $`\mathcal W`$ is not of this form on a chain with $`L>R`$: its blocks would be $`W(x_1-x_2)=\mu_1(x_1)\otimes\mathbb 1+\mathbb 1\otimes\mu_2(x_2)`$; for $`\lvert x_1-x_2\rvert>R`$ the block is zero, which gives $`\mu_1(x_1)=c\,\mathbb 1`$ and $`\mu_2(x_2)=-c\,\mathbb 1`$, with one constant $`c`$ for all positions (every $`x_1`$ is farther than $`R`$ from $`x_2=L`$ or from $`x_2=-L`$, and $`x_1=0`$ from both; likewise for $`x_2`$); hence $`W\equiv0`$.

### Step 2. The operator on the two bodies (item 1b)

1. The partial expectation of $`C-e_f`$ in $`\chi\otimes\varphi`$ is, by (13.1),

```math
C_{\rm bod}=C^{\rm D}_1+C^{\rm D}_2+U+\sum_{i=1,2}g_i\,\hat x_i\otimes G_i+\kappa\langle X\rangle\,(\hat x_1+\hat x_2).
\qquad\text{(13.2)}
```

2. $`[C_{\rm bod},D_i]=0`$, so the levels are conserved, and $`G_i=M_i\sigma^{(i)}_3`$ on the levels $`k_1,k_2`$. With $`\hat x_1=\hat X_\eta+(1-\eta)\hat r`$ and $`\hat x_2=\hat X_\eta-\eta\hat r`$ the gradient terms $`\Delta:=C_{\rm bod}-C_{\rm pair}`$ are

```math
\begin{aligned}
\Delta&=\hat X_\eta\,\mathcal K+\hat r\,\mathcal D_\eta,\qquad \mathcal K:=g_1M_1\sigma^{(1)}_3+g_2M_2\sigma^{(2)}_3+2\kappa\langle X\rangle,\\
\mathcal D_\eta&:=(1-\eta)\,g_1M_1\sigma^{(1)}_3-\eta\,g_2M_2\sigma^{(2)}_3+(1-2\eta)\,\kappa\langle X\rangle .
\end{aligned}
\qquad\text{(13.3)}
```

3. Well-definedness (M6). A replacement of the listed terms that leaves $`C`$ unchanged leaves $`\langle\chi\otimes\varphi\vert C\vert\chi\otimes\varphi\rangle`$ unchanged. It can change the listed $`C_f`$ by a multiple of $`\mathbb 1`$, hence $`e_f`$: $`C_{\rm bod}`$ is fixed by $`C`$, $`\chi`$, $`\varphi`$ up to an additive real constant, which drops out of everything below. Let $`b(x_1,x_2)`$ be the block of $`C_{\rm bod}`$ that is diagonal in both positions, an operator on the internal labels. For $`\lvert x_1-x_2\rvert>R`$ it is $`(1+g_1x_1)G_1+(1+g_2x_2)G_2+\kappa\langle X\rangle(x_1+x_2)`$ up to the constant. $`G_1`$ and $`G_2`$ are traceless and act on the labels of one body each, so the three parts are linearly independent: $`\kappa\langle X\rangle`$ is the normalized trace of $`b(x_1+1,x_2)-b(x_1,x_2)`$ (both arguments in that region), $`g_1G_1`$ is its traceless part, and $`G_1`$ is the traceless part of $`b(0,x_2)`$ on the labels of $`b_1`$; likewise for $`b_2`$.
   - $`g_1`$, $`g_2`$, $`\kappa\langle X\rangle`$: fixed by $`C_{\rm bod}`$, well defined, independent of $`\eta`$. They are the coefficients read from $`C_{\rm bod}`$.    - The operator that multiplies $`\hat r`$: fixed by $`C_{\rm bod}`$ once $`\eta`$ is given. It depends on the convention, $`\mathcal D_\eta=\mathcal D_0-\eta\,\mathcal K`$, and is independent of $`\eta`$ iff $`\mathcal K=0`$, i.e. (as $`M_i>0`$) iff all three gradients vanish.

### Step 3. First-order velocities from a bound level (item 2a)

```math
\qquad\text{(13.4)}
```

and the same formula with $`V_i\to\hat r`$ gives the first-order change of $`\bar m_1-\bar m_2`$. On a finite chain all operators are bounded; the long-chain limit is F4, with (13.5).
2. In the fibres $`\Phi_g(s)`$ is $`g\,e^{-iE_bs}\psi_P`$, and $`\hat X_\eta`$ acts as $`i\partial_P`$ ($`g`$ has compact support in $`\mathcal P_0`$), so

```math
\Delta\,\Phi_g(s)\ \longleftrightarrow\ e^{-iE_b(P)s}\bigl[s\,E_b'\,g\,\mathcal K\psi_P+\xi_P\bigr],\qquad
\xi_P:=i\,g'\,\mathcal K\psi_P+i\,g\,\mathcal K\psi_P'+g\,\hat r\,\mathcal D_\eta\psi_P ,
\qquad\text{(13.5)}
```

a vector of $`\mathfrak h`$ with $`\int\mathrm dP\,\lVert\xi_P\rVert^2<\infty`$ (F2, F3, $`g\in C^1`$ with compact support).
3. Split (13.5) along $`\psi_P`$ and in $`Q_P\mathfrak h`$. On $`\psi_P`$ the propagator is $`e^{-iE_b(\lambda-s)}`$. On $`Q_P\mathfrak h`$, $`\int_0^\lambda\mathrm ds\,e^{-i(H-E_b)(\lambda-s)}=-iR_P\,\mathcal U_P(\lambda)`$ and $`\int_0^\lambda\mathrm ds\,s\,e^{-i(H-E_b)(\lambda-s)}=-i\lambda R_P+R_P^2\,\mathcal U_P(\lambda)`$.
4. Part along $`\psi_P`$. $`\langle V_i\rangle_P=E_b'`$ by (11.7), and $`\langle\mathcal K\rangle_P`$, $`\langle\hat r\mathcal D_\eta\rangle_P`$ are real. The terms with $`\lambda^2`$ and with $`\mathcal D_\eta`$ are imaginary and drop out. With $`2\,\mathrm{Re}\langle\psi_P\vert\mathcal K\vert\psi_P'\rangle=\partial_P\langle\mathcal K\rangle_P`$ the rest is $`\lambda\int\mathrm dP\,E_b'\,\partial_P\bigl(\lvert g\rvert^2\langle\mathcal K\rangle_P\bigr)=-\lambda\,\langle E_b''\langle\mathcal K\rangle\rangle_g`$ (partial integration).
5. Part in $`Q_P\mathfrak h`$. The term $`-i\lambda R_P`$ gives $`-\lambda\int\mathrm dP\,\lvert g\rvert^2E_b'\,2\,\mathrm{Re}\langle V_i\psi_P\vert R_P\vert\mathcal K\psi_P\rangle`$. Since $`V_1-V_2=i[H(P),\hat r]`$ and $`(H-E_b)\psi_P=0`$,

```math
\begin{aligned}
R_PV_1\psi_P&=R_PV_\eta\psi_P+i(1-\eta)\,Q_P\hat r\psi_P,\qquad R_PV_2\psi_P=R_PV_\eta\psi_P-i\eta\,Q_P\hat r\psi_P,\\
2\,\mathrm{Re}\,\langle V_i\psi_P\vert R_P\vert\mathcal K\psi_P\rangle&=-\partial_P\langle\mathcal K\rangle_P\qquad(i=1,2).
\end{aligned}
\qquad\text{(13.6)}
```

The second line holds because $`\partial_P\langle\mathcal K\rangle_P=-2\,\mathrm{Re}\langle\mathcal K\psi_P\vert R_P\vert V_\eta\psi_P\rangle`$ (F2) and $`\langle Q_P\hat r\psi_P\vert\mathcal K\psi_P\rangle=\langle\hat r\mathcal K\rangle_P-\langle\hat r\rangle_P\langle\mathcal K\rangle_P`$ is real ($`[\hat r,\mathcal K]=0`$).
6. Result, for $`i=1,2`$ and every $`\lambda`$:

```math
\boxed{\;v^{(1)}_i(\lambda)=\lambda\,\langle\alpha\rangle_g+\rho_i(\lambda)\;},\qquad \alpha(P):=-E_b''(P)\,\langle\mathcal K\rangle_P+E_b'(P)\,\partial_P\langle\mathcal K\rangle_P ,
\qquad\text{(13.7)}
```

```math
\alpha=g_1\alpha_1+g_2\alpha_2+\kappa\langle X\rangle\,\alpha_X,\qquad
\alpha_i(P)=-M_i\bigl[E_b''\,\langle\sigma^{(i)}_3\rangle_P-E_b'\,\partial_P\langle\sigma^{(i)}_3\rangle_P\bigr],\qquad \alpha_X(P)=-2E_b''(P),
\qquad\text{(13.8)}
```

```math
\begin{aligned}
\rho_i(\lambda)&=-2\,\mathrm{Re}\int\mathrm dP\,\Bigl[i\lvert g\rvert^2E_b'\,\langle V_i\psi_P\vert R_P^2\,\mathcal U_P(\lambda)\vert\mathcal K\psi_P\rangle+g^{*}\langle V_i\psi_P\vert R_P\,\mathcal U_P(\lambda)\vert\xi_P\rangle\Bigr],\\
\lvert\rho_i(\lambda)\rvert&\le\tau_i\,\mathsf B_g,\qquad \mathsf B_g:=\frac4{\Gamma_g}\Bigl[\mathsf b\Bigl(\frac{\tau_{\min}}{\Gamma_g}+\lVert g'\rVert_2+\max_{I_g}\lVert\psi_P'\rVert\Bigr)+\mathsf d_\eta\max_{I_g}\lVert\hat r\psi_P\rVert\Bigr].
\end{aligned}
\qquad\text{(13.9)}
```

The bound uses $`\lVert V_i\rVert\le\tau_i`$, $`\lvert E_b'\rvert\le\tau_{\min}`$, $`\lVert\mathcal U_P\rVert\le2`$, $`\lVert\mathcal K\rVert\le\mathsf b`$, $`\lVert\mathcal D_\eta\rVert\le\mathsf d_\eta`$ and $`\int\lvert g\rvert\lvert g'\rvert\le\lVert g'\rVert_2`$.
7. Status. Both parts have the same coefficient $`\langle\alpha\rangle_g`$ of the growing part. The rests of the two parts are not equal on the class, and $`\rho_i(0)=0`$: they differ in the runs of the numerical sets S2–S5 and of S1 with $`g_1`$ only (section "Code"; at values of $`\lambda`$ within the range of each run, where the first-order separation changes), and they coincide under the conditions of Step 4.3, where $`\bar m_1-\bar m_2=0`$ (set S1 with the common or the plain gradient). The rest is bounded for all $`\lambda`$, in particular for all $`\lambda\ge0`$, under the conditions of the class alone: the level is isolated on the compact $`I_g\subset\mathcal P_0`$ ($`\Gamma_g>0`$) and $`g\in\mathcal G(\mathcal P_0)`$ ($`\lVert g'\rVert_2<\infty`$). $`\rho_i`$ does not depend on $`\eta`$ or on the phase of $`\psi_P`$; the single terms of the bound do. By (11.5) and F2, $`\langle\sigma^{(i)}_3\rangle_P=\partial E_b/\partial M_i`$ and $`\partial_P\langle\sigma^{(i)}_3\rangle_P=\partial E_b'/\partial M_i`$. Numerical check: section "Code".

### Step 4. Mean label separation (item 2b)

1. The computation of Step 3 with $`V_i\to\hat r`$ ($`\langle\hat r\rangle_P`$ is real; (13.6) is not used) gives, for every member of the class of item 2,

```math
\begin{aligned}
(\bar m_1-\bar m_2)^{(1)}(\lambda)&=\lambda\,\langle\beta\rangle_g+\sigma(\lambda),\qquad \lvert\sigma(\lambda)\rvert\le\max_{I_g}\lVert\hat r\psi_P\rVert\;\mathsf B_g,\\
\beta(P)&:=-\langle\mathcal K\rangle_P\,\partial_P\langle\hat r\rangle_P-2E_b'(P)\,\mathrm{Re}\,\langle\hat r\psi_P\vert R_P\vert\mathcal K\psi_P\rangle ,
\end{aligned}
\qquad\text{(13.10)}
```

where $`\sigma`$ is the first line of (13.9) with $`V_i\psi_P\to\hat r\psi_P`$. There is no term $`\propto\lambda^2`$: the first-order relative velocity $`\rho_1-\rho_2=\langle\beta\rangle_g+\dot\sigma`$ has no growing part. This holds alike for the common gradient and for $`g_1\neq g_2`$.
2. Bounded case (sufficient condition). Let $`W(-r)^{*}=W(r)`$ for all $`r`$ (complex conjugate of the matrix in the branch basis); every diagonal on-site binding has this property. The antiunitary $`(\Theta\psi)(r):=\psi(-r)^{*}`$ then commutes with $`H(P)`$ for every $`P`$ and $`\eta`$ ($`\Theta S\Theta^{-1}=S^\dagger`$ in (11.1)) and with $`\mathcal K`$, and $`\Theta\hat r\Theta^{-1}=-\hat r`$. For a simple level $`\Theta\psi_P=e^{i\phi}\psi_P`$, hence $`\langle\hat r\rangle_P=0`$ for all $`P`$ and $`\langle\hat r\psi_P\vert R_P\vert\mathcal K\psi_P\rangle\in i\mathbb R`$. So $`\beta\equiv0`$, and the first-order change of the separation is $`\sigma(\lambda)`$: bounded for all $`\lambda\ge0`$, for all values of the gradients.
3. Vanishing case (sufficient condition). Let $`\tau_1=\tau_2`$, $`M_1=M_2`$, $`W(-r)=\mathsf S\,W(r)\,\mathsf S`$ with $`\mathsf S`$ the exchange of the two branch labels, and $`g_1=g_2`$ (any $`\kappa\langle X\rangle`$). The exchange $`\Sigma`$ of the labels of the two bodies then commutes with $`C_{\rm bod}`$ and with the joint shift, $`\Sigma\Phi_g=\pm\Phi_g`$ (simple level) and $`\Sigma\hat r\Sigma=-\hat r`$. Hence $`\bar m_1-\bar m_2=0`$ for all $`\lambda`$, to all orders in the gradients.
4. Otherwise. For a binding without the property of 2, $`\beta`$ need not vanish: for the numerical control (`code/v6/pair_fall_check.py`, set S5) $`\langle\beta\rangle_g\neq0`$ for each of the three gradients (from the numerical level), so by (13.10) the first-order separation grows linearly in $`\lambda`$; the run of that set shows the slope over its range $`\lambda\le400`$ ($`\lambda\le600`$ for $`g_1`$ only): the internal structure of the level follows the first-order change $`-\lambda\langle\mathcal K\rangle_P`$ of the total label. This rests on that numerical control, not on an analytic example. With the property of 2 and without the conditions of 3 the change is bounded for all $`\lambda`$ (item 2 of this step) and need not vanish: in the runs of the sets S1–S4 (same file; $`\lambda\le3000`$ for S1–S3, $`\lambda\le200`$ for S4), except S1 with the common or the plain gradient, it is not zero and stays bounded over the range of the run. ### Step 5. Drift acceleration (items 2c, 2d)

1. The first-order acceleration is $`a^{(1)}_i=\mathrm dv^{(1)}_i/\mathrm d\lambda=\langle\alpha\rangle_g+\dot\rho_i(\lambda)`$. Its rest is the derivative of (13.9) under the integral ($`H(P)`$ is bounded, $`R_P\,\partial_\lambda\mathcal U_P=i\,Q_Pe^{-i(H-E_b)\lambda}`$, and $`\int\mathrm dP\,\lvert g\rvert\,\lVert\xi_P\rVert<\infty`$):

```math
\dot\rho_i(\lambda)=2\,\mathrm{Re}\int\mathrm dP\,\Bigl[\lvert g\rvert^2E_b'\,\langle V_i\psi_P\vert R_P\,e^{-i(H(P)-E_b)\lambda}\vert\mathcal K\psi_P\rangle-i\,g^{*}\langle V_i\psi_P\vert Q_P\,e^{-i(H(P)-E_b)\lambda}\vert\xi_P\rangle\Bigr],\qquad
\lvert\dot\rho_i(\lambda)\rvert\le\tfrac12\,\tau_i\,\Gamma_g\,\mathsf B_g .
\qquad\text{(13.27)}
```

The bound uses the estimates listed under (13.9), $`\lVert e^{-i(H-E_b)\lambda}\rVert=1`$ and $`\int\lvert g\rvert\,\lVert\xi_P\rVert\le\mathsf b\,(\lVert g'\rVert_2+\max_{I_g}\lVert\psi_P'\rVert)+\mathsf d_\eta\max_{I_g}\lVert\hat r\psi_P\rVert`$; it holds for all $`\lambda`$. So $`\dot\rho_i`$ is bounded for every packet of the class. It is not shown to be small, and its decay for $`\lambda\to\infty`$ is neither derived nor excluded; in the numerical sets it is not negligible over the range of $`\lambda`$ used (section "Code": on half of the range the mean rates deviate from $`\langle\alpha\rangle_g`$ by up to $`1.2\cdot10^{-2}`$). Its window average is a difference quotient of the velocity, exactly: $`\overline{a^{(1)}_i}=\langle\alpha\rangle_g+[\rho_i(\lambda_1+\Lambda)-\rho_i(\lambda_1)]/\Lambda`$, so $`\lvert\overline{a^{(1)}_i}-\langle\alpha\rangle_g\rvert\le2\tau_i\mathsf B_g/\Lambda`$ for all $`\lambda_1\ge0`$, $`\Lambda>0`$.
2. Sharp total label. Class of sequences $`\mathcal S(P_0,I)`$: packets of $`\mathcal G(\mathcal P_0)`$ with supports in one compact interval $`I\subset\mathcal P_0`$, mean $`P_0`$ and $`\delta P\to0`$. Taylor's formula with $`\int(P-P_0)\lvert g\rvert^2=0`$ gives $`\lvert\langle\alpha\rangle_g-\alpha(P_0)\rvert\le\frac12\max_I\lvert\alpha''\rvert\,\delta P^2`$. Hence, with the window first and the sharp label second, for both parts:

```math
\boxed{\;a^{\rm drift}(P_0):=\lim_{\delta P\to0}\,\lim_{\Lambda\to\infty}\overline{a^{(1)}_i}=g_1\,\alpha_1(P_0)+g_2\,\alpha_2(P_0)+\kappa\langle X\rangle\,\alpha_X(P_0)\;},\qquad \alpha_i=-M_i\Bigl[E_b''\,\frac{\partial E_b}{\partial M_i}-E_b'\,\frac{\partial E_b'}{\partial M_i}\Bigr],\quad\alpha_X=-2E_b'' .
\qquad\text{(13.11)}
```

Here $`E_b'=\langle V_i\rangle_P`$ (11.7), $`\partial E_b/\partial M_i=\langle\sigma^{(i)}_3\rangle_P`$ (11.5), and $`E_b''=-\eta^2\langle A_1\rangle_P-(1-\eta)^2\langle A_2\rangle_P-2\mathcal S_\eta(P)`$ (11.9).
3. The other order (item 2(c) as restricted on 2026-10-11). Fix a window $`[\lambda_1,\lambda_1+\Lambda]`$, $`\lambda_1\ge0`$, $`\Lambda>0`$. The class of sequences is $`\mathcal S(P_0,I)`$ of 2, unchanged: it restricts $`\lvert g\rvert^2`$ (support in $`I`$, mean $`P_0`$, $`\delta P\to0`$) and leaves the phase of $`g`$ free. A class that restricts the phase is another class; no statement is made for it. All statements are for one part $`i`$. The item asks a determination (the other order exists and agrees with the defining order, or it does not exist) for two groups, the members with $`g_1=g_2=0`$ and the levels of items 3 and 4 at $`P_0=0`$; for the other members it asks three things, marked (i), (ii), (iii) below.
   - The fixed-window quantity. In (13.9) the packet enters through $`\lvert g\rvert^2`$ and through $`g^{*}g'=\tfrac12(\lvert g\rvert^2)'+i\,\mathsf j_g`$ with $`\mathsf j_g:=\mathrm{Im}(g^{*}g')`$, a continuous function with support in $`I`$. Put $`F_{i,\lambda}(P):=\langle V_i\psi_P\vert R_P\,\mathcal U_P(\lambda)\vert\mathcal K\psi_P\rangle`$ and $`\mathsf A_{i,\lambda}(P):=-2\,\mathrm{Re}\bigl[iE_b'\langle V_i\psi_P\vert R_P^2\,\mathcal U_P(\lambda)\vert\mathcal K\psi_P\rangle+\langle V_i\psi_P\vert R_P\,\mathcal U_P(\lambda)\vert\,i\mathcal K\psi_P'+\hat r\mathcal D_\eta\psi_P\rangle\bigr]`$, both analytic in $`P\in\mathcal P_0`$ (F2, F3) and independent of $`g`$. With $`\xi_P`$ of (13.5) and a partial integration of the term with $`(\lvert g\rvert^2)'`$, (13.9) is $`\rho_i(\lambda)=\langle\mathsf A_{i,\lambda}-\partial_P\mathrm{Im}F_{i,\lambda}\rangle_g+2\int\mathrm dP\,\mathsf j_g\,\mathrm{Re}F_{i,\lambda}`$. With the difference quotient of 1, exactly:

```math
\overline{a^{(1)}_i}=\langle\alpha\rangle_g+\frac1\Lambda\Bigl[\langle\mathsf G_i\rangle_g+2\int\mathrm dP\;\mathsf j_g(P)\,\mathsf f_i(P)\Bigr],\qquad
\mathsf f_i(P):=\mathrm{Re}\bigl[F_{i,\lambda_1+\Lambda}(P)-F_{i,\lambda_1}(P)\bigr],\quad
\mathsf G_i(P):=\Bigl[\mathsf A_{i,\lambda}(P)-\partial_P\mathrm{Im}F_{i,\lambda}(P)\Bigr]_{\lambda=\lambda_1}^{\lambda=\lambda_1+\Lambda} .
\qquad\text{(13.28)}
```

   $`\mathsf f_i`$ depends on the level, the gradients and the window; it does not depend on $`\eta`$ or on the phase of $`\psi_P`$ ($`\mathsf G_i`$ and $`\mathsf j_g`$ do; the bracket of (13.28) does not). The joint shift by $`n`$ labels, $`g\to g\,e^{-inP}`$, is the special case $`\mathsf j_g\to\mathsf j_g-n\lvert g\rvert^2`$.
   - (i) Existence at a fixed window (necessary and sufficient condition, for every member of the class of item 2). For every sequence of the class $`\langle\alpha\rangle_g\to\alpha(P_0)`$ and $`\langle\mathsf G_i\rangle_g\to\mathsf G_i(P_0)`$ (continuous functions on $`I`$; Chebyshev's inequality). The last term of (13.28) is not controlled by $`\lvert g\rvert^2`$. For a real number $`c`$ put $`\tilde g:=g\,e^{ic\phi}`$ with $`\phi(P):=\int_{P_0}^P\mathsf f_i`$. Then $`\lvert\tilde g\rvert^2=\lvert g\rvert^2`$, so $`\tilde g`$ belongs to a sequence of the class whenever $`g`$ does, and $`\mathsf j_{\tilde g}=\mathsf j_g+c\,\mathsf f_i\lvert g\rvert^2`$ changes the window average by $`\frac{2c}\Lambda\langle\mathsf f_i^2\rangle_g`$. $`\mathsf f_i`$ is analytic on the interval $`\mathcal P_0`$. If it does not vanish identically, its zeros are isolated and $`\langle\mathsf f_i^2\rangle_g>0`$ for every packet; with $`c=c_n`$ chosen along a given sequence, the window averages then diverge, or converge to any prescribed real number. This holds also if $`\mathsf f_i(P_0)=0`$; then $`c_n\to\infty`$ (the phase of the packet varies faster and faster). If $`\mathsf f_i\equiv0`$ on $`\mathcal P_0`$, the limit is $`\alpha(P_0)+\mathsf G_i(P_0)/\Lambda`$ for every sequence. Hence: at a fixed window the limit $`\delta P\to0`$ exists for every sequence of $`\mathcal S(P_0,I)`$ iff $`\mathsf f_i\equiv0`$ on $`\mathcal P_0`$ (equivalently on $`I`$).
   - (ii) A necessary condition (N) for the other order. "The other order exists on the class" means: for one $`\lambda_1`$ and all $`\Lambda`$ beyond some $`\Lambda_0`$ the limit $`\delta P\to0`$ exists for every sequence of the class, and the limit $`\Lambda\to\infty`$ of the result exists. Define $`c_i`$ by (13.29); it is analytic in $`\lambda`$ ($`H(P)`$ is bounded) and in $`P`$, and $`\mathsf f_i(P)=c_i(\lambda_1;P)-c_i(\lambda_1+\Lambda;P)`$. If the other order exists, $`c_i(\lambda;P)=c_i(\lambda_1;P)`$ for all $`\lambda\ge\lambda_1+\Lambda_0`$, hence for all $`\lambda`$ (analyticity). The mean of $`c_i(\lambda;P)`$ over $`\lambda\in[0,T]`$ tends to zero for $`T\to\infty`$ (mean ergodic theorem: $`H(P)-E_b(P)`$ has no kernel on $`Q_P\mathfrak h`$, simple level), so this constant is zero. So the other order exists on $`\mathcal S(P_0,I)`$ only if (N): $`c_i\equiv0`$ on $`\mathbb R\times\mathcal P_0`$, where

```math
c_i(\lambda;P):=\mathrm{Re}\,\bigl\langle V_i\psi_P\big\vert R_P\,e^{-i(H(P)-E_b(P))\lambda}\big\vert\mathcal K\psi_P\bigr\rangle,\qquad
c_i(0;P)=-\tfrac12\,\partial_P\langle\mathcal K\rangle_P,\qquad
\partial_\lambda^2c_i(0;P)=-\tfrac12\,\bigl\langle\,[V_i,[H(P),\mathcal K]]\,\bigr\rangle_P .
\qquad\text{(13.29)}
```

   The value at $`\lambda=0`$ is (13.6); the second derivative is $`-\mathrm{Re}\langle V_i\psi_P\vert(H-E_b)\vert\mathcal K\psi_P\rangle`$ with $`(H-E_b)\mathcal K\psi_P=[H,\mathcal K]\psi_P`$. Conversely, if $`c_i\equiv0`$ then $`\mathsf f_i\equiv0`$ for every window, and the limit $`\delta P\to0`$ exists for every window and every sequence. For a member with $`c_i\not\equiv0`$ there is a $`P_1\in\mathcal P_0`$ with $`c_i(\cdot\,;P_1)\not\equiv0`$; this function of $`\lambda`$ is analytic and, by its vanishing mean, not constant. So for every $`\lambda_1`$ the limit $`\delta P\to0`$ fails to exist on the class for all $`\Lambda`$ outside a set without accumulation point.
   - (ii) A sufficient condition (S), and the spectral form of (N) and (S). For $`P\in\mathcal P_0`$ let $`\Omega_P:=\mathrm{spec}H(P)\setminus\{E_b(P)\}`$, a compact set at the distance $`\Gamma(P)`$ from $`E_b(P)`$, and let $`\nu_{i,P}`$ be the complex measure of (13.31) on the Borel sets $`S\subseteq\mathbb R`$ (F5; $`\mathbb 1_S`$ is the indicator function of $`S`$). It is finite and carried by $`\Omega_P-E_b(P)`$, hence by $`\lvert\omega\rvert\ge\Gamma(P)`$, and with $`R_P=Q_P(H(P)-E_b)^{-1}Q_P`$:

```math
\nu_{i,P}(S):=\bigl\langle V_i\psi_P\big\vert\,Q_P\,\mathbb 1_S\bigl(H(P)-E_b(P)\bigr)\,\big\vert\mathcal K\psi_P\bigr\rangle,\qquad
c_i(\lambda;P)=\mathrm{Re}\int\frac{e^{-i\omega\lambda}}{\omega}\,\mathrm d\nu_{i,P}(\omega),\qquad
F_{i,\lambda}(P)=\int\frac{1-e^{-i\omega\lambda}}{\omega}\,\mathrm d\nu_{i,P}(\omega).
\qquad\text{(13.31)}
```

     - (N) at $`P`$, that is $`c_i(\cdot\,;P)\equiv0`$, holds iff $`\nu_{i,P}(S)=\overline{\nu_{i,P}(-S)}`$ for every $`S`$. Reason: $`2c_i(\lambda;P)`$ is the Fourier transform of the finite measure $`\omega^{-1}\,\mathrm d\bigl[\nu_{i,P}(\omega)-\overline{\nu_{i,P}(-\omega)}\bigr]`$ (F5). Equivalently: $`\mathrm{Re}\,F_{i,\lambda}(P)=0`$ for all $`\lambda`$, because $`\mathrm{Re}\,F_{i,\lambda}=c_i(0;\cdot)-c_i(\lambda;\cdot)`$ and the mean of $`c_i`$ over $`\lambda`$ vanishes.
     - (S) at $`P`$ is: $`F_{i,\lambda}(P)=0`$ for all $`\lambda`$. It holds iff $`\nu_{i,P}=0`$: the Fourier transform of $`\omega^{-1}\nu_{i,P}`$ is then constant, so this measure is a multiple of the point mass at $`\omega=0`$, where it has no mass.
     - (S) on $`\mathcal P_0`$ is sufficient for the other order, which then agrees with the defining order. Proof: $`\mathsf f_i\equiv0`$ for every window, $`\mathsf G_i=\mathsf A_{i,\lambda_1+\Lambda}-\mathsf A_{i,\lambda_1}`$, and $`\lvert\mathsf A_{i,\lambda}(P)\rvert\le\frac{4\tau_i}{\Gamma(P)}\bigl[\mathsf b\,\bigl(\frac{\tau_{\min}}{\Gamma(P)}+\lVert\psi_P'\rVert\bigr)+\mathsf d_\eta\lVert\hat r\psi_P\rVert\bigr]`$ for all $`\lambda`$ (the estimates listed under (13.9)). By (13.28) the limit $`\delta P\to0`$ is $`\alpha(P_0)+[\mathsf A_{i,\lambda_1+\Lambda}(P_0)-\mathsf A_{i,\lambda_1}(P_0)]/\Lambda`$ for every sequence, and the limit $`\Lambda\to\infty`$ of that is $`\alpha(P_0)`$: the two limits commute.
     - (S) implies (N). A member with (N) and without (S) has $`\mathrm{Re}\,F_{i,\lambda}\equiv0`$ for all $`\lambda`$ and $`\mathrm{Im}\,F_{i,\lambda}\not\equiv0`$ for some $`\lambda`$.
   - Where (N) and (S) coincide. The level has **no mirror pair at $`P`$** if no two points $`E`$, $`E'`$ of $`\Omega_P`$ satisfy $`E+E'=2E_b(P)`$, that is, if the compact sets $`\Omega_P-E_b(P)`$ and $`E_b(P)-\Omega_P`$ are disjoint. Then (N) at $`P`$ implies $`\nu_{i,P}=0`$: for a Borel set $`S\subseteq\Omega_P-E_b(P)`$ the set $`-S`$ does not meet the carrier of $`\nu_{i,P}`$, so $`\nu_{i,P}(S)=\overline{\nu_{i,P}(-S)}=0`$. If the level has no mirror pair at one $`P_1\in\mathcal P_0`$, it has none on a neighbourhood of $`P_1`$ (the two sets have a positive distance, and $`E_b(P)`$ and the spectrum of $`H(P)`$ move continuously, F5). For a member with (N), $`F_{i,\lambda}(\cdot)`$ then vanishes on that neighbourhood for every $`\lambda`$, hence on $`\mathcal P_0`$ (it is analytic in $`P`$): (S) holds. With the two bullets before:

```math
\text{no mirror pair at one }P_1\in\mathcal P_0:\qquad
\text{the other order exists on }\mathcal S(P_0,I)\iff c_i\equiv0\ \text{on}\ \mathbb R\times\mathcal P_0\iff F_{i,\lambda}\equiv0\ \text{on}\ \mathcal P_0\ \text{for all}\ \lambda ,
```

5. Clock levels (item 3c). With $`u=4\lvert w\rvert\mathfrak m_1\mathfrak m_2/(\mathfrak m_1+\mathfrak m_2)`$, (13.13) reads

   The remainder is additive: by (13.13) each of the three terms of the numerator carries its own relative remainder $`r_1,r_2,r_X=O(\epsilon^2)`$.    - Comparison: a free part falls alike on all levels iff $`\langle X\rangle=0`$ (4.16). "At a fixed $`u`$" in the other statements of this step refers to one pair of levels; no statement is made for pairs of levels compared at one common value of $`u`$, which for all pairs of levels would need a binding that depends on the levels, by this monotonicity (out of scope).
